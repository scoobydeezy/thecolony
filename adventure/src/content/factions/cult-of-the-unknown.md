history: |-
  The Cult of the Unknown began as a loose fellowship of Seekers who believed the Colony had mistaken mystery for danger. While others debated the burden as a political or moral problem, these early adherents became captivated by the strange experiences surrounding the ritual itself. They collected dreams, survivor accounts, moments of déjà vu, and stories of places where reality seemed thin, believing that understanding required encounter rather than explanation. Many wandered the Duskwood in silence, fasted, meditated, or sought solitude in places marked by lingering absence, convinced that the Unknown was not merely to be feared, but approached.

  The Cult came to see the Veiled manifestations not as invaders, but as Pilgrims—beings moving toward a destination the Colony had forgotten. Their gatherings gradually transformed into acts of pilgrimage and devotion. Pilgrim's Rest became a sanctuary for travelers, mystics, and the spiritually wounded, with rooms kept prepared for guests who might never arrive. The Duskwood Shrine marked places where the boundary between worlds felt especially thin, drawing those willing to seek revelation beyond the safety of the Colony.

  The Offering Chest grew from the Cult's imperfect understanding of fractured objects: belongings of the Forgotten that sometimes carry enough emotional weight to wound reality itself. Believing the power lay in sacrifice rather than history, Cult pilgrims began surrendering treasured possessions in the hope that a freely chosen absence might create the same connection to the Unknown. Their greatest collection, the Face of the Unknown, preserves hundreds of paintings, sculptures, carvings, and tapestries created by those who claim to have glimpsed the Veiled. No two depictions agree, and the Cult considers that disagreement itself to be sacred. Every vision is believed to reveal only one fragment of a reality too vast for any single witness to comprehend.

  Over generations, seeking became ritual, ritual became faith, and faith became doctrine. Today, the Cult of the Unknown offers hospitality to pilgrims, refuge to those whose experiences defy explanation, and reverence toward mysteries the rest of the Colony would rather contain. Its members believe that surrender, encounter, and devotion draw them closer to the truth hidden beyond the Veil, even if they cannot yet understand what waits there.
