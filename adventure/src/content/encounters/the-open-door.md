## At the Table

**Objective:** make welcome compatible with a guest’s real authority to leave. **Situation:** an open, ordinary inn; a neighborhood happily unavailable. **Track:** each affected guest’s At Home / Deeply At Home state and meaningful changes of circumstance. **Recovery:** Maelyn’s offering ceremony supplies an internal opportunity even if everyone critically fails. **No combat or maze is required.**

## Mechanical Reference

Use :ref[The Welcome That Never Ends]{#hazard.welcome-that-never-ends} for provisional DC, recovery results and counteraction. The rules below explain the situation and the actual limits on agency.

### Encounter Identity
**The quiet catastrophe.** Under Totality, the entire Pilgrim Quarter has gone silent. By the time Darkwing investigates, its streets are empty, its shops unattended, and every resident has entered Pilgrim's Rest. Inside, living townsfolk and Veiled Pilgrims celebrate together. No one is bound, threatened, or attempting escape. **Everyone feels too profoundly at home to want to leave.**

This is a **location-Wound / Context + Choice encounter** built as an open-ended narrative problem with one consistent supernatural constraint, not a room maze, a mass-evacuation board, or a predetermined series of social skill checks. The front door is open. It works. The danger is the loss of meaningful desire to depart.

### Narrative Container — The Sanctuary Remembering Itself
Pilgrim's Rest is an ordinary inn and active Cult sanctuary. Generations of hosts have prepared rooms for the unknown, received people the Colony rejected, and poured genuine hopes, fears, care, attention, and expectation into the same place. The building has accumulated **supplied mnemonic Weight** through those repeated lived experiences. It is now an independently resonant **location-Source**; it need not be the byproduct of a ruptured Source or Scar.

Totality intensifies Recognition until the inn begins reconstructing its remembered **Context of hospitality** as a universal truth. Existing Veiled drawn from *genuine* mnemonic Sources find an unusually receptive setting, while ordinary living people are welcomed just as thoroughly. The inn does **not** create Forgotten people out of nothing. Its Wound makes no distinction between a Veiled Pilgrim and an ordinary neighbor: both are guests, and both are subject to the same emotional influence.

- **Genuine memory:** People came seeking welcome, received it, and sometimes truly found peace and belonging.
- **False interpretation:** Anyone who has been welcomed belongs here so completely that they could never wish to leave.
- **Reconstructed condition:** The sanctuary magnifies authentic comfort, relief, safety, curiosity, or hope until competing desires and obligations seem unimportant.
- **Truth condition:** Hospitality can remain meaningful when a guest freely chooses to leave and the host willingly relinquishes their claim to keep that person. **Receiving someone does not grant authority to determine what they want.**

No invisible barrier blocks the exit. The location-Source is the **inn itself**, including the practice of reception encoded in its remembered use; the Offering Chest is a significant *instrument and evidence* of Cult tradition, **not** automatically the sole Source or an invented keystone switch.

### Delayed Discovery — The Absence of an Alarm
At the onset of Totality, the other crises call attention to themselves: EGRESS ruptures, Milltown's siren sounds, Old Town's Broken Bell rings. The Pilgrim Quarter does the opposite.

The Rest fills with Pilgrims, and people gather to witness the event. Family members, vendors, Cult workers, curious onlookers, and those looking for missing neighbors come inside. Each has an intelligible initial reason to enter; do **not** portray a magical sweep or compulsory teleportation that empties the district. Once welcomed, none has any desire to return to their ordinary work. The effect cascades by social attraction and unanswered absences until the whole neighborhood is inside.

**Default reveal:** Allow approximately one other major Act III encounter's worth of in-world time after Totality begins before the Party is told how conspicuously quiet the quarter has become. A messenger never returns; scheduled Cult assistance does not arrive; someone realizes there are no witnesses on the Pilgrim Quarter streets. If Darkwing proactively investigates sooner, honor that choice and portray the population still gathering rather than imposing a mandatory delay.

When the Party arrives at the default moment:

- No one walks the streets. Shops and stalls have been abandoned in the middle of ordinary work. Windows and neighborhood doors may stand open; nothing looks violently evacuated.
- Pilgrim's Rest is brightly lit, its entrance plainly open. From the street, the crowded celebration is strangely silent or muffled; crossing the threshold reveals a sudden fullness of talk, song, clinking dishes, and ordinary human warmth. This acoustic contrast is local atmosphere, not another navigation puzzle or a universal Wound rule.
- The interior contains living townsfolk, Cult adherents, and pure-black Veiled silhouettes receiving food, company, and attention. Some Pilgrims remain strange or unsettling, but not every Incursion is hostile. The Cult has genuine cause to celebrate.
- **No one has tried to leave.** Do not stage a trapped guest struggling at the doorway. If players ask why a shop was abandoned or whether anyone has considered going home, answers are entirely sincere: *“Later.” “Tomorrow.” “There's no need to rush.”*

The inn retains its **normal, knowable architecture**. No looping rooms, phantom wings, repeatable hallways, or hidden exit puzzle: those belong to *Witness Me*, not this encounter.

### Opening State — The Perfect Welcome
Maelyn hosts what she believes may be the fulfillment of generations of Cult devotion. She is gracious and genuinely joyful, neither a secret villain nor knowingly controlling her guests. Tarn is present, unusually relaxed or less watchful than normal; the Wound can influence the most conscientious people without erasing their established values. Their stated responsibilities outside remain perfectly intelligible, but no longer feel urgent.

Darkwing is offered food, drink, a place beside the fire, conversation, and inclusion. On entering the inn, **each PC makes the initial high-difficulty Will save** against **the Welcome That Never Ends**. Record results separately. The actual hazard level, DC, traits, counteract details, and action-level implementation belong in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page; the encounter-level result meanings are fixed here:

| Entry save | Table-facing consequence |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Fully resists; may leave and re-enter without another Welcome save for this encounter. |
| **Success** | Resists; may depart normally, but must save again if returning after leaving. |
| **Failure — At Home** | Feels deep peace and contentment. Can think, converse, investigate, fight, and make choices normally, but intending to **depart voluntarily** requires a further Will save. A failed attempt does not yield unlimited immediate retries. |
| **Critical failure — Deeply At Home** | Is completely engrossed in the sanctuary's belonging. Departure does not seem desirable or worth contemplating; no routine departure saves are available. **Meaningful internal contradictions and special recovery opportunities can still grant a new save.** The character is not stupefied, confused, controlled, or taken out of the player's hands. |

**Departure attempt after ordinary failure:** A critical success frees the guest and grants encounter immunity; a success permits departure and the influence ends outside; a failure leaves the guest At Home; a critical failure deepens the condition to Deeply At Home. Repeating identical declarations is not a new meaningful attempt. A genuinely changed circumstance can create a new opportunity.

**Not a default PF2e condition:** *At Home* and *Deeply At Home* are named states of this specific Wound, not replacements for **fascinated** or **controlled**. Do not apply those conditions' unrelated penalties or action restrictions. Respect appropriate immunities, spells, counteract methods, and the player's description of their character within the actual magical constraint.

### What the Players Can Observe
These are freely accessible signs of the phenomenon, not clues that require a particular skill check:

- The front entrance remains physically passable. The Wound alters **motivation**, not geometry.
- People have not forgotten their obligations. They know the Colony is in crisis, but honestly do not mind postponing their response.
- Contentment is not restricted to Cult adherents, people who accepted a rite, or Veiled. It affects anyone welcomed by the Rest, including skeptical visitors and Darkwing.
- The peace is built on *real* desires—rest, safety, company, belonging—not completely invented emotions. Those desires have been made disproportionately important.
- The previously experienced Cult lesson from *The Shape of Someone* applies: a **genuine act of reception** does not prove that the Cult's conclusion about what it has received is true. There, welcoming didn't establish **Form**; here, happiness doesn't establish **Choice**.

Appropriate Recall Knowledge or examination can distinguish environmental mental influence from a locked-door effect, identify Pilgrim's Rest as a location-Source / Wound, or clarify the Context/Choice contradiction. **No roll produces the mandatory right speech or overrides an NPC's agency.**

### The First Internal Contradiction — Maelyn's Offering
During the continuing celebration, **Maelyn invites the guests to contribute something genuinely precious to the Cult's Offering Chest**. She does this because welcoming the Pilgrims is a momentous occasion, not because she realizes she has trapped anyone or because the GM needs a rescue scene. The ceremony arises naturally from her established practice of relinquishing valued possessions so their relationships may continue elsewhere.

This is the encounter's **guaranteed, entirely internal Moment of Clarity**—especially important if the whole Party critically failed its entry save.

> *“Not what you have finished with. Something whose absence you will feel. And remember: an offering must be freely given.”*

Each PC is given an actual choice: whether to offer something meaningful, refuse, negotiate where it will go, or challenge what the ceremony means. **Do not require players to surrender permanent treasure or reward only compliance.** A sincere refusal can be just as truthful an act of Choice as a sincere offering.

For affected PCs, a personally meaningful conflict between imposed contentment and a genuine attachment, promise, responsibility, or refusal permits a **special recovery Will save even after an initial critical failure**. This is not a generic once-per-round reroll. A critical success ends the Welcome; a success improves its severity or allows that particular consequential choice to be acted upon; lesser results retain pressure without removing the PC's ability to participate. Final degrees and any bonuses belong in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page.

**The offering does not automatically cure the Wound.** The Chest can fill with treasured things while everything remains inside the Rest: the Cult has sincerely *received*, but what has been *released*? A player who notices that distinction has found the central metaphor in a physical, established Cult object. The Chest may be moved, its contents transferred, a gift declined, or an offering redirected without requiring it to be destroyed or treated as the Wound's mechanical core.

### Maelyn's Blind Spot — The Intended Narrative Route
Maelyn's existing flaw is **mistaking willingness for readiness**. She respects consent but often interprets sincere willingness as adequate proof that an encounter can be borne safely. Everyone in Pilgrim's Rest appears sincerely happy to remain. That is precisely why she does not see the Wound as dangerous.

The Party's objective, if they follow this route, is **not** to beat a Diplomacy DC, shame her for hospitality, or tell her the correct answer. It is to place two of her own commitments in genuine conflict and let *her* recognize what follows:

1. **She believes offerings are meaningful only when freely given.** The recipient may accept, refuse, keep, pass on, or eventually leave with a gift. Tarn's old ancestral sword, freely surrendered and now waiting in a prepared room, is a pre-existing, personal illustration.
2. **She believes everyone at the Rest willingly belongs there.** She has taken contentment as proof of unconstrained desire, even when people have neglected real relationships and obligations outside.
3. **The contradiction:** *If a visitor may refuse a treasured offering, why can a guest not truly choose to refuse a home?* If the possibility of refusal has been removed, is the welcome still an offer?

When Maelyn **personally recognizes** that the guests' desire to remain is being supplied or overwhelming their own competing commitments, she gets **her own special Will save**, regardless of her initial degree of influence. She is not the Party's puppet: her realization must arise from a meaningful observation, decision, or conversation rather than a player's declaration that she is convinced.

- On a strong result, she can act against the Welcome and confront what her sanctuary is doing.
- On a partial result, she recognizes the contradiction but still struggles to imagine departing; her recognition can inform another honest decision.
- On a weak result, the insight need not disappear. Preserve what she has actually learned and permit a further save only upon genuinely different evidence or choice.

**The Last Offering** is the preferred climax. The person who has relinquished possessions for centuries recognizes that the thing she holds most tightly is the right to keep her sanctuary—and its welcome—forever. She offers up **her claim to determine whether others must remain**. She may choose to walk out through the wide-open door herself. Or she may sincerely invite a guest to leave, acknowledge their decision, and help them go without treating it as a rejection. A guest might also decline her hospitality and be recognized as equally worthy of it. The critical element is **real, consequential permission for departure**, not physical ownership of the inn, a sacrificial payment, a magic phrase, or the exact identity of the first person to cross the threshold.

The intended proof is a **completed act of hospitality**: someone has truly been welcomed; that person freely chooses departure; someone who could claim them as a permanent guest knowingly honors and releases that choice. No second mandatory truth-check roll is required after the Party earns and enacts that distinction. The building's false Context becomes untenable, and the Wound's imposed priority collapses.

If Maelyn steps outside and then voluntarily comes back in, this is a particularly strong epilogue: she has not renounced her home or its guests. **She has chosen it.** An optional line: *“You're always welcome here. And you're always free to go.”* The Colony's farewell, *“The light remains,”* can carry its established meaning without functioning as a magic password.

### An Open-Ended Situation, Not a Persuasion Puzzle
The intended route is **not exclusive**. Players may discover other truthful ways to confront or interrupt the false Context. Adjudicate what their approach actually establishes:

| Player approach | Consistent GM response |
|---|---|
| **Ask guests about work, family, danger, promises, or a planned departure** | Their memories remain intact. A concrete personal contradiction may grant a special recovery save; generic exhortations to “snap out of it” do not. |
| **Offer something, decline something, give it onward, or involve the Offering Chest** | Honor meaningful Choice rather than the ritual's surface appearance. The decisive question is whether acceptance, refusal, and departure are actually respected. |
| **Engage Maelyn or Tarn about consent, preparedness, and hospitality** | Their established convictions provide leverage; neither exists to utter the answer on command. Tarn's protective responsibility and relinquished sword are useful but not mandatory. |
| **A PC or guest freely chooses to leave and is knowingly released** | This can supply the same true Context as Maelyn's intended climax. Do not require Maelyn to pass a save if the Party has already demonstrated the full truth by other credible means. |
| **Counteract, suppress, protect, or bypass the mental influence** | Allow ordinary PF2e magic and abilities to work within their scope. Freeing individuals or creating a brief window of agency is not necessarily the same as stabilizing the whole location-Source. |
| **Carry, teleport, or otherwise physically remove a guest** | There is no spatial prohibition; removal is possible, though an affected person may resist. Outside the active influence, the guest can recover. Forced removal is **rescue**, not proof of freely chosen departure or the Wound's underlying resolution. |
| **Start a fight, make a frightening scene, or start a fire** | Real danger and ordinary consequences occur; the Wound may make guests prioritize protecting their beloved sanctuary instead of fleeing it. Do not make violence an automatic universal Will-save dispel. Destruction risks injured civilians, damaged Cult capacity, and uncontrolled mnemonic Weight. |
| **Attack, empty, destroy, or relocate the Offering Chest** | The Chest is an authentic Cult channel of passage but **not** the location-Source itself. Some actions may reveal the contradiction, interrupt a ceremony, or create a new real choice; destroying the Chest alone does not solve the Wound. |
| **Solve by an entirely different demonstration of Context and Choice** | If the act genuinely proves welcome can coexist with a person's effective authority to depart, allow truthful Stabilization. Do not demand the planned dialogue or prop sequence. |

### When Everybody Succumbs
**Do not introduce a messenger, injury, or new emergency outside the door solely to rescue the scenario.** The encounter's recovery opportunities must emerge from its own people and practices.

- Every PC still acts and decides in all matters **except** the Wound's imposed priority concerning leaving. They can talk, question, disagree, experiment, make gifts, and refuse invitations. Even a Party of critical failures remains playable.
- Maelyn's Offering Chest ceremony proceeds as a natural part of the celebration, regardless of Darkwing's save results. Ask each player what they actually value and how they respond; this grants the first special recovery opportunity when it provokes meaningful dissonance.
- Don't make the ceremony an infallible forced cure. If saves go badly, use consequences *inside* the Rest—an offering someone will not surrender, a gift another guest refuses, the discovery that nothing placed into the Chest has meaningfully passed onward, Maelyn's changing interpretation, or a PC's serious proposal to relinquish a claim—as further, distinct opportunities for choices and saves.
- **A successful Will save is not a mandatory gate to intellectual discovery or taking all useful actions.** PCs can establish the contradiction, change an arrangement, challenge a false claim, or engage Maelyn while still under influence. True restoration of the Context can break the environmental effect without requiring every individual to roll themselves free first.
- If the Party chooses to remain and never challenges the Wound, the crisis resolves **unattended**: the Cult's staff and neighborhood stay contentedly unavailable while other Totality deadlines pass. Do not turn a lost opportunity into an invisible rescue or endless repeated rolls.

### Restoration, Aftermath, and Act III → Finale Handoff
A truthful act of **welcome, free departure, and willing release** corrects the Rest's false Context. Its imposed contentment dissipates. People regain the practical significance of the work and relationships awaiting them beyond the sanctuary; some leave and some freely stay. Veiled guests need not be driven out, killed, or individually Anchored merely to end this Wound. The sanctuary continues to provide valid Context for real Pilgrims supported by genuine Sources. Stabilizing this Wound does **not** automatically Anchor every guest, erase the accumulated memory of welcome, or fix the rest of Totality.

The immediate operation is **restoring the Cult's ability to send people and resources out of Pilgrim's Rest**. Pilgrim's Rest may be **Lost as a functioning Asset** without the building burning. Its **single Cult-front state** simultaneously determines +2/+1/+0 Finale Pressure and full/half/no Form intervention in *The Burden*. Insight + Relationship determine whether that intervention helps or harms Darkwing; there is no extra Operational Integrity roll or Asset gate.

| Party commitment / outcome | Cult front / Pilgrim's Rest / Finale Pressure | Cult finale capacity (direction by Insight + Relationship) |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaningful direct intervention that resolves the Wound while preserving the sanctuary** | **Stable / +2** | Full: Assist removes a complete Form family, or Interfere grants the Composite four actions each round. |
| **Viable delegation to an available, capable champion or team** | **Damaged / +1** | Half: Assist removes one Form, or Interfere grants an extra action on alternating rounds. Delegation consumes real capacity elsewhere. |
| **Ignored / allowed to remain enthralled through the deadline** | **Lost / +0 (functionally unavailable)** | No organized Cult intervention. The inn can remain physically standing; its people and capability are unavailable. |

These are prepared triage outcomes, not immunity from direct player decisions. A deliberately burned or ruptured Rest might truly be structurally destroyed even during direct play. An extraordinary successful offscreen intervention may change the baseline if the campaign has already established the means. The broader Act III operation and concurrent clocks remain governed by :ref[the faction rules]{#rule.faction-goals}.

**GM outcome flags:** (1) Did the Wound achieve truthful Stabilization? (2) Which PCs and significant guests remain influenced or have departed? (3) Did Maelyn recognize her blind spot and exercise Choice? (4) Cult front = Pilgrim's Rest condition = +2/+1/+0 Finale Pressure; (5) Cult Insight and Relationship entering *The Burden*; (6) any genuine Veiled guests/Anchors whose circumstances changed independently.

### Systems Exercised
**Supplied mnemonic Weight; location-Source → Wound under Totality; false Context applied equally to living people and Veiled; emotion and meaningful Choice; genuine desire versus unconstrained consent; existing Cult practice as internal counterexample; truthful release without a compulsory NPC speech or combat; physical rescue versus environment-wide Restoration; five concurrent Act III crises and selective intervention.**

### Remaining Mechanical Implementation
Place the hard Welcome DC, hazard level/traits, repeated-departure save details, counteract/suppression options, and any special recovery-save adjustments in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page or the campaign's applicable hazard-rules source when the Party's level and resources are fixed. This encounter is deliberately complete **without** an additional clock, maze, numeric puzzle, or custom combatant.
