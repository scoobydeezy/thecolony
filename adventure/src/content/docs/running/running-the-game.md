A session should change the Colony through something the Party understands and chooses. Prepare pressures, evidence, and people pursuing objectives. Leave the answer to the players.

## Prepare a Situation

Begin with what has changed since last session and what the latest [Colony Turn](/docs/running/colony-turn/) established. Choose an active ambition or unresolved pressure that the Party is likely to meet.

For the coming session, identify its practical adventure, entering question, faction perspective, mystery, and character relationship. Decide what can become visible, where a meaningful choice arises, and what persists afterward. A route through an archive, a contested evacuation, and a conversation about a remembered promise should each have consequences beyond completing the scene.

Suggested session numbers are pacing aids. Do not make a new session force an unfinished scene to end, or advance an offscreen ambition without its governing procedure.

## Let Experience Precede Explanation

Introduce a small, concrete instance before explaining a campaign-wide truth. The cloth toy makes incomplete reconstruction dangerous and personal. Repaired objects and continuing debts let the Party experience absence before anyone can name it.

Give essential truths enough evidence to be recovered through play. A failed approach should expose a new need, change the situation, or invite a different method; it should not make the rest of the campaign unknowable. Use the :ref[Identity recovery rule]{#rule.identity-and-stabilization} where mnemonic evidence is involved.

Distinguish an NPC's account from established fact. Sincere people can misunderstand an event. Give the Party ways to compare testimony with objects, records, behavior, and place rather than making one faction's exposition the answer key.

## Play People Who Protect Something

Before an important conversation, decide what the NPC protects, what they fear losing, what assumption they have stopped questioning, and what might make them reconsider. Their entry supplies those motives; their current circumstances determine how they express them.

Faction membership is a community, not a script. Members can share an aim while disagreeing about a method. Let someone be helpful, difficult, frightened, or wrong for a reason the Party can understand.

Let NPCs change. Death, defection, lost credibility, or an unexpected friendship can transform an institution. Protect narrative momentum by finding an appropriate new bearer for a necessary function, while preserving the consequences of losing the old one. Do not automatically transfer faction assignments.

## Give Conflict an Objective

Before combat, establish what every side wants beyond defeating its enemies. Someone may be protecting a person, delaying a transfer, securing evidence, escaping, or buying time for a ritual. Decide what they will risk and when they might retreat, negotiate, or surrender.

The objective's outcome matters more than a body count. A spared rival, destroyed bridge, lost record, or public rescue changes what the Colony can do afterward. Apply the played result even when it disrupts the expected sequence.

Veiled combat makes unresolved pressure tangible. Dispersal can create an opportunity for truth; it does not erase the burden beneath the manifestation. Keep that distinction clear without interrupting action for a lecture.

## Make Props Evidence

Use objects with a history of use: repairs, worn edges, annotations, missing counterparts, or a shape that fits an unfamiliar hand. Tie an important prop to a person, institution, Asset, or obligation.

Let its state change. The Party may see it in another home, recover it from storage, or notice what a new owner has misunderstood. Preserve the evidence already established; changed circumstances should deepen its meaning rather than replace it with a more convenient clue.

## Ask Without Steering

The :ref[Observer]{#npc.silas-venn} can expose an assumption or ask who will inherit a proposed solution's cost. He is not the campaign's authority.

Use a question that helps the Party examine its own answer: who is accountable if this fails, what circumstances would require revision, or what happens to the person who refuses? Once asked, listen. Repeating the challenge until the players agree turns inquiry into a demand.

## Carry Consequences Forward

After a scene, record the concrete changes: who controls an Asset, who is injured or unavailable, what agreement was made, which evidence was Restored, and what obligation remains. The app records live state; session notes preserve the reasons and promises that make it meaningful.

Show consequences without handing down a philosophical grade. An institution may become more useful and more dangerous at once. The Colony should respond honestly to what the players built.

## Before the Finale

Check that optional routes have not become mandatory keys, major promises have earned payoff routes, and surviving factions have shown both their value and their limits. The Party must have enough evidence and freedom to construct its own answer.

Hope lies in people accepting responsibility and remaining willing to revise what they build. The campaign does not need to prove that their final institution will remain wise forever.
