## Overview

The previous Keeper was the girl’s father, though neither she nor anyone else remembers that he existed. Before the Reading, he was a cartographer and civic surveyor whose work helped document the Colony’s roads, properties, forgotten boundaries, and impossible absences.
Eighteen months ago, he completed the Reading and disappeared from memory.

## Personality

Patient, observant, and more comfortable correcting a map than confronting an institution. He distrusted clean lines, official certainty, and any survey that claimed the world was simpler than the people living within it.
He taught his daughter that an incorrect map was not worthless. It showed where understanding had failed.

## Motivation

Map the Colony honestly, prepare his daughter to find her own way, and carry the burden he believes his understanding obligates him to accept.

## Primary Protection

**His daughter’s ability to find her own way.**
He wanted to prepare her for the world without deciding her path for her. The cartographer’s compass was an ordinary gift, not a final keepsake. He expected to continue teaching her.

## Fears

That his daughter will inherit a world mapped by other people’s certainty and lose the ability to choose her own path.

## Blind Spot

He believed that understanding the Ritual’s cost made him uniquely responsible for accepting it.
Like the Keepers before him, gratitude toward the lives he encountered became willingness. He did not recognize that choosing the burden himself still allowed the Colony to avoid carrying it with him.

## Narrative Role

The father explains why a good and informed person might willingly complete the Reading.
His choice humanizes the Ritual without vindicating the institution. When he is finally recovered as a person, he offers testimony, love, and perspective—not a verdict about what his daughter must do.

## Portrayal Notes

Patient and observant, with the quiet concentration of someone correcting a map rather than winning an argument. He notices the human cost hidden by clean abstractions. With his daughter, warmth should feel ordinary and unfinished rather than elegiac.

## Pressure Response

He becomes still and exact, abandoning explanation for a single practical correction or protective action.

## Signature Quote

*“A map doesn’t have to be right to show you where it got lost.”*

## Signature Object

A civic survey map covered in practical corrections, uncertain boundaries, and notes about the people official maps omit.

## Connection to the Observer

He helped provide the physical and civic foundation for the Observer’s map of the Colony. The Observer studied institutions; the cartographer studied the places and people those institutions shaped.

His absence survives through habits she cannot explain:
* Her fascination with bad maps.
* The symbols she uses for roads and homes.
* The professional compass she carries.
* Household records implying another adult.
* Routes through the Colony that feel familiar.
* Skills she does not remember learning.
