*Results Pending* begins with an investigation and ends with the Party taking responsibility for an answer it must build. The Colony is a working society, its protections have real value, and its inherited solution has a real human cost. Your task is to make those truths playable without deciding what the players should conclude.

## Before the First Session

Read [The Campaign at a Glance](/docs/running/campaign-at-a-glance/) for the premise and three acts, then [GM Truths](/docs/running/gm-truths/) for the secret beneath them. [The Reading and Its Branches](/docs/running/reading-and-branches/) explains the persistent choice at the end of Act I.

Use [How to Use This Book](/docs/running/how-to-use/) for navigation and markers. Prepare the first scenes from [Act I](/docs/act-1/), and choose a few details from [The Colony](/docs/setting/) that make the place worth knowing.

## Before a Veiled Encounter

Read [The Veiled: A Practical Primer](/docs/veiled/primer/) to understand the sequence of danger, evidence, and resolution. [The Seven Components of Identity](/docs/veiled/seven-components/) explains what each investigation teaches. The encounter itself supplies its required evidence, openings, and consequences; linked rule entries own the reusable procedures.

## Between Sessions

[The Colony Turn](/docs/running/colony-turn/) turns recorded consequences into the next session's starting world. [Faction Operations and the Act III Fronts](/docs/running/faction-operations/) connects those changes to playable objectives and the finale.

Use [Running Investigations, Relationships and Consequences](/docs/running/running-the-game/) to prepare the next meaningful decisions, [Promises and Threads](/docs/running/promises-and-threads/) to keep long-term relationships alive, and [Character Progression and Rewards](/docs/running/progression-and-rewards/) to distinguish practical rewards from permissions the campaign must never require.

## The GM's Responsibility

Create conditions. Let the players create meaning. Give each important person something to protect, let consequences persist, and preserve the possibility that a sincere answer is incomplete. Neither the Observer nor a successful encounter speaks the campaign's final verdict.
