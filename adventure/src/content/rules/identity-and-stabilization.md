## Definition

**Stabilization** is the process of Recovering truthful evidence of a forgotten life, Restoring it through an available opening, and completing it through Acceptance.

### The Seven Components

Identity constrains what reality can reconstruct. Each component corrects a different kind of error.

| Component | Establishes |
|---|---|
| Name | Which individual life a fragment belongs to. |
| Relationship | Who and what mattered to the person. |
| Pattern | Obligations, dependencies, ownership, debts, and continuing material consequences. |
| Memory | What the person perceived, felt, feared, understood, or intended. |
| Form | The truthful outward identity and boundaries of the person or people being reconstructed. |
| Context | When, where, and under what circumstances the information was true. |
| Choice | What the person chose, refused, accepted, resisted, or had imposed upon them. |

The encounter identifies the available Identity paths and evidence. Which components can supply sufficient Meaning is set by the Veiled's Reconstruction and Resonance types; see :ref[Reconstruction and Resonance Paths]{#rule.reconstruction-and-resonance}.

## Procedure

### 1. Recover Evidence

**Recover** means the Party knows something true. A Fragment may be an object, physical feature, habit, phrase, record, emotional response, or other evidence of a life. Most Fragments are evidence; an encounter such as a Chorus may also give them a physical role on the battlefield.

Evidence can emerge from investigation, conversation, exploration, combat, changing Resonance, or observing a manifestation's response. Every required component must have a local recovery route. Optional investigations and reusable tools improve access; they do not grant permission to finish the campaign.

There is no universal memory skill. Choose a skill, spell, ability, or other method that addresses the evidence: a medical observation, a repaired object, a performance, a record of civic duty, or a conversation may each matter. Use the encounter's normal level-based DC and its specific instructions.

| Evidence | Suggested difficulty |
|---|---|
| No meaningful lead | No meaningful attempt yet |
| One weak lead | Very Hard |
| One strong lead or two weak leads | Hard |
| Several useful fragments | Standard |
| Strongly constrained identity | Easy |
| Effectively explicit answer | Very Easy or automatic |

Unless the encounter supplies another result, success recovers the component; critical success also gives an advantage, connection, clue, or safer method. Failure requires new evidence or a meaningfully changed approach before retrying. Critical failure adds an encounter-specific complication, such as endangered evidence or escalation. Do not permanently hide essential truth or invent false history as the ordinary result of a failed check.

### 2. Restore Identity

**Restore** means reality has incorporated recovered truth. Restoration normally occurs during a :ref[Stabilization Window]{#rule.stabilization-window}, or another opening the encounter makes legible. Recovery can happen before that opening.

Present the truth in a way that addresses the reconstruction: speak a recovered Name, restore objects to their actual relationship, replay an authentic Memory, or establish the historical Context of a room. The method determines the check, if one is needed. Do not demand a redundant check when the established fiction makes the result inevitable.

A Restore interaction within a Window adds Reconstruction Pressure normally unless the encounter explicitly exempts it. Once Restored, Identity remains true through later reconstructions. The Veiled cannot contradict it. Apply the encounter's visible consequence immediately: an ability stops, a boundary becomes clear, a route opens, or a trapped person is released. A changed phase does not reset recovered progress.

### 3. Acceptance

When sufficient truthful Meaning makes the false reconstruction untenable, ask a focused question such as **“Who were they?”** or **“What did the Colony mistake for the whole person?”** Establish the fixed historical constraints and let the players give the life those facts imply meaning. Their answer becomes canon within those constraints.

Acceptance is not a skill check and adds no Reconstruction Pressure. It recognizes a life as distinct from its false reconstruction. It need not declare the person good, agree with their choices, discover every historical fact, or volunteer the Party to carry their Weight.

Acceptance completes **Stabilization**. That false reconstruction cannot recur. The manifestation may disappear, become coherent and non-hostile, settle into its Source, or reveal the next layer of an encounter. A layered encounter may reserve Acceptance for the complete person at its end.

## Interactions

The final hostile reconstruction ends when Stabilized. Its Weight still needs a destination; use :ref[Anchoring and Resolution]{#rule.anchoring-and-resolution}. Lack of an Anchor does not revoke the truth already Restored.
