## Overview

The Consortium’s senior representative in the Colony is an obsessive administrator drowning in impossible paperwork. Debts have no debtors. Houses have no owners. Contracts bear signatures no one recognizes. Since arriving, less than eighteen months ago, he has been trying to prove that the errors are connected.
He is correct. He is simply terrible at explaining why.

## Personality

Frantic, sleep-deprived, and perpetually three conclusions ahead of everyone else. His office is a maze of ledgers, string, annotations, contradictory diagrams, and clerks who have learned not to move anything.
He talks too quickly, interrupts himself, forgets what others do not yet know, and produces crucial evidence from seemingly unrelated piles. Beneath the chaos, he is extraordinarily competent.

## Motivation

He believes civilization survives through logistics: food, roads, contracts, inventories, and obligations.
The Colony’s forgetting continually breaks those systems. He wants to understand where people, property, and responsibility go when identity disappears—not from metaphysical curiosity, but because someone still has to balance the books.

## Primary Protection

**The Colony’s practical continuity.**
People should remain fed, housed, supplied, and connected even when the records insist they do not exist.

## Fears

That the Colony’s practical systems will collapse because ownership, obligation, and responsibility can no longer be assigned to anyone real.

## Blind Spot

He can map where every consequence went without understanding what those consequences mean.
To him, any problem becomes manageable once it can be recorded, assigned, and routed. A balanced ledger does not necessarily produce justice.

## Narrative Role

He inverts the Party’s expectations of Aspis and introduces the campaign through comic chaos rather than solemn exposition.
At first, he appears to be connecting unrelated accounting errors. By Totality, the Claims Registry reveals that he has unknowingly mapped homes, dependents, obligations, inheritances, and relationships left behind by the Forgotten.
He has not solved the mystery.
He has documented its shape.

## Portrayal Notes

Speak quickly, interrupt yourself, and jump between papers as though everyone else already knows the intermediate conclusions. The comedy comes from presentation, never incompetence. When the crisis becomes practical, the noise suddenly resolves into extraordinary clarity.

## Pressure Response

The frantic digressions vanish. He becomes startlingly concise, assigns responsibilities, and reveals that every chaotic pile has already been prioritized.

## Signature Quote

*“I know the numbers are wrong. The problem is that they are wrong consistently.”*

## Signature Object

A battered master ledger filled with impossible balances, cross-references, and scraps of colored string.

## Performance Inspiration

Charlie Day (Pepe Silvia)

## Relationship to the Party

He greets the Party with overwhelming relief. At last, someone outside the Colony might look at his evidence without already having decided which parts are impossible.
He is honest, generous with resources, and almost incomprehensible. What initially sounds like paranoid rambling gradually proves to be the campaign’s most complete record of the Colony’s absences.
