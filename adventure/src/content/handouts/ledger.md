## Reference Dossiers — Actual Player Evidence

*Give an excerpt only when its actual `X` lead is followed or the party finds the source through play. These are authentic Witnesses records. Their facts are always true, but some archivists' hypotheses are explicitly marked as hypotheses.*

### `ACC.041` — House of Abiding, post-Reading intake

Objects `H.200.3.041` (blue cup) and `H.200.3.042` (meal tin) were delivered in a shared transfer. Intake notes list **several different rooms** and no individual owner. The cup was found among personal effects awaiting attribution; the tin came from a **communal meal-storage cupboard**. This accession establishes a transfer, not a household or a single source person. The cup had two tea leaves in its bowl at collection.

### `ACC.058` — Market-side lodging

Objects `H.196.1.058` (red mitten) and `H.196.1.059` (spinning top) were collected from the same child's lodging after the occupant could no longer be reliably named. The caretaker had observed the child playing with the top while wearing the mitten. These two objects **really are connected**. Their connection is not evidence of a relationship to the Spider.

### `ACC.113` — Maelyn's Cult delivery

The Cult's `CULT.DON` channel combines multiple offerings received over several days. Two recorded subsets arrived together: a bundle from the **twenty-five-piece sewing kit `SET.140`** containing the brass thimble, and separately gathered flowers and offerings including the petal bottle. An Offering Chest is a collection channel; it does not imply that donors or original owners were the same person.

### `SET.140` — The twenty-five-piece sewing kit

A bag of twenty-five trade and devotional needlework components was donated through Maelyn. The thimble's handed fit and pronounced band of wear suggest extensive personal use, while many other components are standard communal-pattern tools. The kit's last custodial holder is known; the original ownership of individual pieces is **not** established from the donation alone.

### `SET.219` — Paired cup and saucer

The blue teacup once belonged to a **two-piece tea service** with a matching small saucer, separately accessioned. The saucer is **not** among the twelve testing candidates. This set relation does not identify the forgotten person who used the cup.

### `PAT.017` — Eight-return binding and decorative loops

The eight-return binding is an old craft technique used widely in the Pilgrim Quarter: a repeated sequence of eight passes terminating in a slightly uneven crossing. The same profile is recorded on the child's mitten (`H.196.1.058`), the linen handkerchief (`H.196.5.023`), and the repair on the blue teacup's handle (`H.200.3.041`). Similar loopwork appears in devotional embellishment, but the motif is **not exclusive** to Nana Anadi or to one maker. Matching a technique establishes familiarity with a tradition, **not** a single owner.

### `LOC.021` — East craft annex, Pilgrim Quarter

The east annex had **a communal outer door**, multiple workrooms, and an interior personal needlework station referred to as **Station W-3** in its own account books. Historical plans distinguish the public entry from W-3's locking cupboard. Many customers, apprentices, and neighbors crossed the common rooms; being associated with this site alone is not evidence of owning objects at W-3.

### `DOC.217` — House of Abiding, earlier blue-cup custody

A transfer clerk appended this earlier provenance statement to the post-Reading cup entry: “Blue-patterned cup, repaired handle, tea leaves retained. Previously kept behind the personal divider of **Station W-3** at east annex, beneath the loom-side shelf. Repeatedly seen there before transfer.” The clerk marked the older note **supported but not witnessed personally**. The cup's later path through the House of Abiding is secure; the intermediate transfer is only partially known.

### `DOC.086` — Needlework tool-roll record

The annex keeper's tool roll separates **communal shared needles** from the **hand-fitted brass thimble retained at Station W-3**. Its outer eight-loop etching and narrow wear band match `H.198.7.113`. A later kit inventory collected that thimble with other tools before donation through `SET.140`. Thus the thimble was **used and kept at W-3**, independently of its Cult donation route.

### `DOC.142` — Personal cupboard lock and altered key

An annex repair docket describes a private cupboard beneath **Station W-3** with a lock recut for **two shortened bit channels**. The measurements match the altered profile of `H.207.2.008`. The key itself later surfaced in the **House of Abiding pre-Reading floorboards**. The courier or event that carried it between these places was not established. The lock was not the communal annex door.

### `DOC.061` — Child's lodging and workmanship

The mitten and spinning top were both documented with the same child's possessions and were seen in use there. The mitten's thumb binding matches the broader `PAT.017` technique taught by several Pilgrim Quarter needleworkers. A child's use of an item doesn't prove the repairer was its original owner.

### `DOC.093` — Household mending commission

A linen handkerchief embroidered with three stars was repaired as **a client commission** through the annex's shared workbench. The eight-return seam echoes `PAT.017`, and the donor's devotional needlework was not considered unusual. The handkerchief was **returned to its client** before its later unrelated accession. A worksite connection is not private ownership of Station W-3's personal effects.

### `DOC.064` — Outer annex door work

The carved hinge came from the east annex's **communal entrance**, which had four recorded paint layers and was repaired in a period distinct from the lock changes to the personal W-3 cupboard. The hinge and private key refer to **different doors** despite both being associated with `LOC.021`.

### `DOC.052` — Communal meal-storage tin

The lunch tin's seven interior scratches were recorded in a House of Abiding **shared** food-storage area. They provide no evidence that the tin belonged to the blue teacup's owner. Their shared `ACC.041` refers only to an institution's transfer batch.

### `DOC.056` — Cobbler's oversize-footwear commission

A cobbler specializing in large-bodied customers used a stiff working glove while cutting oversized soles with a broad crescent-shaped leather knife. The glove was repaired with orange waxed cord. A needlework motif on the cuff was a **trade decoration**, not evidence of the Spider craftsperson's personal workbench.

### `DOC.081` — Community serving record

The long-handled pewter spoon matches serving utensils used in a communal kitchen. The original cook's repetitive work was providing food to many people. The spoon's shallow bowl is suited to portioning stew rather than to violence.

### `DOC.119` — Optical examination instructions

A market optician's instructions tell clients to compare alternate lens settings by answering **“One, or two?”** The particular spectacles in the Hall have a repair using **four parallel wire turns**, unlike the eight-return textile pattern. Resemblance to many eyes is not evidence of hostility.

### `DOC.066` — Devotional flower offerings

Small bottles of dried petals were donated from the Offering Chest by many visitors; some were bound with **eight-loop ornamental cords** familiar from Nana Anadi's local craft traditions. The petal bottle `H.198.7.114` is from a mixed floral donation and lacks evidence of being a personal tool at Station W-3. Religious decoration alone does not establish original ownership.

### `MAT.029`, `MAT.031`, `MAT.032` — Material comparisons

`MAT.029`: leather and waxed-cord methods for heavy cobbler work. `MAT.031`: pewter and food residue on kitchen tools. `MAT.032`: repairs to brass-and-glass spectacle frames. These references classify materials and manufacture; they are not ownership registries.

### `Z.1548`, `Z.012`, `Z.022`, `Z.031` — Prior isolated incidents

`Z.1548`: wooden top rotated spontaneously. `Z.012`: leather glove briefly curled its fingers without a hand. `Z.022`: a spectacle lens tilted of its own accord. `Z.031`: petals suspended briefly in the sealed bottle. These demonstrate that benign and potentially alarming manifestations can arise from **non-Spider** objects. They do not rank current danger or automatically identify a main Source.
