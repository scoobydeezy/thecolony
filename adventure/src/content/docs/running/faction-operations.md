An operation is a person trying to change or protect something. Start with the champion's objective and the actual state of the Colony, then prepare the moment when the Party can meaningfully affect that work.

Use :ref[Faction Goals and Champions]{#rule.faction-goals} for goal rules and :ref[Act III Front Outcomes]{#rule.act-three-fronts} for the shared finale procedure. Individual encounters own their clocks, opposition, and resolution conditions.

## Preparing an Operation

Establish who can execute or direct it, what they want, what stage their goal has reached, and what person or Asset is at stake. Give the method a visible form: a convoy, a guarded archive, a meeting, an evacuation, or machinery being prepared.

Identify the opposition and the places where intervention could change the result. If the operation has a deadline, make its approach legible in the fiction. Prepare the unattended consequence rather than inventing a punishment after the Party decides to go elsewhere.

A champion's absence changes the means available. Another person can inherit a narrative function, but a faction goal stays Dormant until its assignment is explicitly changed.

## The Change at Totality

Act II asks the Party to investigate partial truths. Under Totality, those same methods face emergencies. The five fronts test what their factions can preserve and bring to the final reconstruction. :ref[Act III Front Outcomes]{#rule.act-three-fronts} owns the front, faction, front goal, front Asset and Maintain guard for each.

These are concurrent crises, not five compulsory stops. The Party can intervene, arrange meaningful delegation, or leave a front unattended. Its decision uses time, people, and resources that may be needed elsewhere. The result comes from the encounter and the established situation.

## Bring Forward the Existing State

Read each front's opening through the actual goal stage, available champion, and Asset condition. Earlier damage and lost access matter. A prepared response is useful without settling the emergency before it is played.

Use the :ref[front outcome rule]{#rule.act-three-fronts} once for each result. It connects the front's condition to its finale contribution without adding a second integrity track. Keep separate consequences separate: a loose Dragon, Sundara's older Wound, or the Spider's fourth object does not become an extra front score.

## Cooperation Is Not a Completion Key

Relationships determine how surviving capabilities act. An ally may integrate its method into the Party's design; an opponent may use the same method to pursue a competing answer. Let the Party negotiate, redirect, protect, seize, or work around a capability as the actual situation allows.

Required Identity remains recoverable locally. The Party does not need unanimous faction support to reconstruct Aletheia. Building the society it wants afterward may depend much more deeply on other people's consent.

Aspis and the Order remain active participants, but they are not additional crisis fronts. Their aid depends on the people, access, and relationships established in play.

## Worked Situations

These examples illustrate the :ref[Colony Turn]{#rule.colony-turn} and :ref[front outcome rule]{#rule.act-three-fronts}; their opening conditions are hypothetical, not changes to the campaign baseline.

**Jorren and the Dead Drop Network.** If Jorren’s eligible operation threatens the network, its active Maintain guard can enter the Shattered’s draw. A drawn guard changes the operation’s difficulty; it does not make a separate opposing roll. An initial advance to Progressing does not damage the Asset before the corresponding outcome occurs. If the network is actually Damaged before Sunny, that prior damage caps the front’s eventual contribution.

**Damaris and Sister Marra.** If Damaris attempts to steal the Ledger and Marra’s protection is drawn, apply the best drawn guard under the operation rule. If the Ledger changes hands through either operations or direct play, update the one Asset record. Do not simultaneously describe it as stolen and safely retained. Loss of control is still a fictional ruling to record; it is not automatically identical to physical destruction.

**Milltown, direct or delegated.** Suppose Dawn has staged rescue teams and its Assembly Hall remains Stable. Darkwing can play the rescue directly and earn the encounter’s actual result. A capable delegate can use the prepared delegated outcome. If nobody responds before an announced deadline, apply the unattended outcome. All three routes feed the same front record; choosing delegation does not create a second roll for the Asset or silently negate the preparation.
