## Overview

The next Keeper is a fifteen-year-old apparent orphan who has only just been Called when the Party arrives. She is being moved into the House of Abiding, celebrated by the Colony, and prepared for a future no one will explain clearly.
Before she is a symbol, sacrifice, or solution, she is a person determined to remain part of her own story.

## Personality

Curious, meddlesome, and socially fearless. She asks personal questions, gives nicknames to people she likes, and dislikes being discussed while standing in the room.
She is emotionally perceptive but often guesses the wrong explanation. In danger, she is brave but inexperienced. When frightened, she becomes quiet rather than funny.

## Motivation

Remain part of her own story and participate honestly in every decision about the burden gathering around her.

## Primary Protection

**Her right to participate in decisions about her own life.**
She can tolerate disagreement more easily than secrecy. Nothing angers her faster than being controlled “for her own protection.”

## Fears

Being reduced to a title, protected out of her own life, or treated as a burden whose only value is what she can endure for others.

## Blind Spot

She treats usefulness as the price of belonging and hardship as proof of maturity.
When others call her young, fragile, or burdensome, she becomes determined to show that she can endure anything. This makes her willingness to sacrifice herself sincere—but never simple.

## Narrative Role

The Keeper is the emotional center of the campaign and the recurring test of whether compassion can become another form of control.
Her agency includes curiosity, mistakes, risk, refusal, and the possibility that she may freely choose a sacrifice the Party desperately wants to prevent.

## Portrayal Notes

Curious, fearless in conversation, and quick to challenge adults who discuss her as though she is absent. Let humor and nicknames disappear when she is truly frightened. Her bravery is real, but it is the bravery of someone who has not yet learned all the ways courage can be used against her.

## Pressure Response

She stops joking and using nicknames. She listens in complete silence, then states what she intends to do without softening it for anyone.

## Signature Quote

*“I am listening. I just don’t agree, which is apparently different.”*

## Signature Object

Her father’s professional cartographer’s compass.

## Performance Inspiration

Hailee Steinfeld

## Notes

Her name represents her arc:
Varenya - means "chosen"
Vara - means "choice"

## Bad Maps

She collects inaccurate, outdated, and contradictory maps. She believes a bad map still reveals what its maker noticed, assumed, or needed.
She becomes fascinated by the Party’s travels and ecstatic upon discovering the annotated map in the Manor. Her instinct later helps her understand the Veiled: they are not accurate people, but incomplete representations built around missing information.

## The Compass

She always carries a professional cartographer’s compass despite having no remembered reason to own one. She spins it while thinking and grips it tightly when anxious.
Her father gave it to her while teaching her mapmaking. She believes she has simply always had it.

## Relationship to the Party

The Party meets her before the title of Keeper completely consumes her identity. She is drawn to them because they represent lives beyond the Colony and because they initially speak to her without already knowing what she is supposed to become.
Her trust depends less upon whether they agree with her than whether they tell her the truth and allow her to act upon it.
