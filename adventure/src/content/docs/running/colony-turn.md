The Colony Turn gives the next session a world that has moved since the Party last saw it. Faction ambitions and personal pressures continue without requiring the GM to choose which dramatic outcome ought to happen.

The complete procedure lives in :ref[The Colony Turn rule]{#rule.colony-turn}. :ref[Faction Goals and Champions]{#rule.faction-goals} defines eligibility, assignments, completion, and resolution tasks. Use those entries at the table; this page explains how to turn the results into preparation.

## Begin With What Actually Happened

Record the previous session's direct consequences before running the Turn: injuries, deaths, agreements, defections, Asset transfers, and any goal outcomes established through play. Check who can still direct an operation. Reassign an unowned goal explicitly if someone has actually taken responsibility for it.

The app owns those live records. Starting values in the book describe the beginning of the campaign, not a second copy of the current Colony.

## Let the Turn Establish the World

The Opening resolves accumulated Momentum signals. The Faction Phase resolves drawn operations against the same starting snapshot. The Character Phase then resolves personal crises and changes. Publish makes the result available for the next session.

Inspect the results before creating scenes. A changed guard pattern may make an archive harder to reach; a damaged route may put a practical limit on a faction's plan. Those are circumstances the Party can respond to, not encounters it must complete in a prescribed order.

## Bring Consequences Into View

Translate a consequential result into something people can observe. If an operation produces public preparation, show the preparation. If it remains secret, show only the evidence its actual effects leave. Do not reveal hidden goals merely to explain a die roll.

Choose the developments relevant to the places and people the Party is approaching. The rest remain true without demanding a scene. A useful Turn leaves a playable world, not a checklist of compulsory interruptions.

## Leave Played Outcomes to Play

A Ready to Resolve task means an ambition has reached a point needing resolution. Defer outcomes that require the Party's intervention, a confrontation, or a consequential choice. Do not accept an assassination or a front's success as bookkeeping when its culmination is the encounter.

Once the session begins, people act and the world changes through play. Record those effects directly; no general faction draw ratifies a victory or supplies an unseen penalty. Use the encounter's authored deadline where one exists.

## The Narrative Handoff

Alongside the app's live state, keep brief session notes on:

- **The Keeper:** Vara's intentions if alive, or the particular residues and recovered relationships if Forgotten.
- **Faction access:** who trusts the Party, what agreements bind them, and which capabilities are usable on what terms.
- **Identity:** lessons actually experienced and evidence already recovered.
- **Open promises:** relationships or clues the campaign still owes a payoff.
- **Weight:** the specific people, objects, places, and institutions currently carrying it.

These notes explain how to play the recorded state. They do not duplicate goal progress or add hidden mechanical tracks. See [Promises and Threads](/docs/running/promises-and-threads/) for the continuity obligations.
