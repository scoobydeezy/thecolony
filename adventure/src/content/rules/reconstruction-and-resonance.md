## Definition

Every Veiled has two independent mechanical classifications in addition to **Class**:

- **Reconstruction** identifies the kind of mistake reality is making as it assembles the missing life.
- **Resonance Type** identifies the kind of surviving truth through which the Source recognizes the present.

Each classification points toward an Identity component that can provide sufficient Meaning.

These are **alternate Restoration paths, not mandatory double locks**.

> **Restoring sufficient truthful Meaning through either path can Stabilize an Incursion.**

**Reconstruction** answers:

> **What mistake is reality making as it gives the missing life a shape?**

**Resonance Type** answers:

> **What true thing did reality recognize first?**

A field description might therefore be:

> **Class II Echo / Relationship**

Every part has mechanical meaning:

- **Class II** describes mnemonic magnitude.
- **Echo** says **Memory** is one viable Restoration path.
- **Relationship Resonance** says **Relationship** is another viable Restoration path.

The taxonomy should become practical battlefield language rather than lore trivia.

## Procedure

1. **Name the field description:** Class, Reconstruction and Resonance Type (for example, Class II Echo / Relationship).
2. **Find both Restoration paths** in the Reconstruction Paths and Resonance Type Paths tables below.
3. **Restore sufficient truthful Meaning through either path**, using :ref[Identity and Stabilization]{#rule.identity-and-stabilization}. Both paths may be recovered. Some encounters may expose only one practical route.
4. **Test the result.** Recognition of the relevant component is not enough by itself: the recovered truth must materially constrain the false conclusion reality is currently making.

## Reconstruction Paths

| Reconstruction | Restoration Path | Structural Problem | What the Party Learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Echo** | **Memory** | One apparent life has behavior or impulse without enough lived interiority. | What did this person actually experience? |
| **Chorus** | **Name** | Several apparent lives have unstable boundaries and can become interchangeable. | Which fragments belong inside which individual life? |
| **Composite** | **Form** | Fragments have been assembled into one or more boundaries that never existed. | What shape or person-boundary has reality constructed incorrectly? |
| **Wound** | **Context** | A place, event, or institution is reconstructing truths in the wrong circumstances. | What actually belonged here, when, and under what conditions? |

### Echo — Memory

An **Echo** is one apparent forgotten life reconstructed around a surviving impulse, routine, task, fear, relationship, or imperative.

It may know:

- protect her;
- finish the work;
- return home;
- keep them out;
- find him;

without retaining enough lived experience to bound that behavior humanely.

Restoring Memory gives behavior an interior point of view.

A useful mechanical pattern is to give an Echo a signature **Imperative** ability. Restoring Memory should permanently alter, suppress, or reinterpret it.

Experienced shorthand:

> **Echo? Find the Memory.**

### Chorus — Name

A **Chorus** presents several apparent people whose identity boundaries are unstable.

The key mechanical distinction is:

> **Many bodies; fragments can migrate between them.**

A Chorus can be modeled using **Corpora**: manifested bodies containing a recognizable identity kernel plus currently attached Fragments.

### Corpora and Identity Kernels

Each Corpus must preserve enough stable information that the Party can recognize a continuing life across changes.

Useful stable signals include:

- ancestry or silhouette;
- a recurring gesture or behavior;
- a grave, object, or place association;
- a repeated emotional or relational cue.

Without stable kernels, fragment migration becomes arbitrary and the encounter stops being solvable.

### Migratory Fragments

A Fragment originates from some life, but before Name boundaries are restored the Chorus may attach it to the wrong Corpus.

Migratory Fragments can include:

- weapons;
- spellcasting or combat techniques;
- clothing or equipment;
- remembered phrases;
- occupations;
- physical features;
- emotional responses;
- other recognizable pieces of Identity.

Dispersing a Corpus does not necessarily remove those Fragments. The encounter may redistribute them among remaining or reconstructing Corpora.

This creates a useful tactical lesson: focus-firing “the caster” can Disperse the body while the **spellcasting Fragment moves somewhere else**.

### Named Boundaries

Restoring a correct Name establishes a stable boundary around one life.

Once a life is Named:

- its rightful Fragments can be recalled to it;
- foreign Fragments are expelled;
- its Fragments can no longer migrate into unrelated Corpora;
- that life can Stabilize or Anchor independently when the encounter permits it.

A Chorus does not always require every member to be Named. Restore enough boundaries to make interchangeability untenable.

Physical table implementations are encouraged when useful. Swappable miniature parts, cards, rings, tokens, or markers can make fragment migration immediately readable. The exact swap/randomization rule is encounter-specific rather than universal.

Experienced shorthand:

> **Chorus? Name them.**

### Composite — Form

A **Composite** is different from a Chorus.

A Chorus has **many apparent people with unstable ownership of pieces**.

A Composite has **one or a few apparent bodies whose very Form is assembled from pieces that should not constitute that identity**.

Its topology is cross-wired at the level of shape and person-boundary.

Restoring Form can:

- remove an ability borrowed from another identity;
- shed a limb, face, age, movement mode, or attack that never belonged there;
- split an impossible body into truthful parts;
- prevent specific fragments from recombining into the same false Form.

Experienced shorthand:

> **Composite? Separate its Form.**

### Wound — Context

A **Wound** is a place, event, institution, or environment attempting to remember.

Its topology is spatial, temporal, procedural, or institutional rather than primarily bodily.

True objects and relationships may all be present while occupying the wrong rooms, years, roles, or circumstances.

Restoring Context should stabilize part of the battlefield.

Possible effects include:

- disabling a hazard zone;
- removing historical terrain;
- revealing a safe route;
- returning a room to the present;
- preventing a living person from being assigned a historical role;
- breaking a false situated Resonance condition.

Experienced shorthand:

> **Wound? Restore Context.**

## Coherence

**Coherence** describes how much mutually compatible information has assembled into a stable reconstruction.

Coherence is a reusable encounter concept, not a universal numeric subsystem.

Increasing Coherence may:

- merge several fragmentary bodies into one person;
- make features or behavior recognizable;
- unlock speech or intelligible testimony;
- expose a new Restoration opportunity;
- change defenses or abilities;
- make an Incursion either more or less dangerous.

Coherence should answer:

> **How much of a mutually compatible person or event can reality currently hold together?**

It should not automatically mean “higher Coherence = lower resistance” or any other fixed modifier. The Hall of Remembrance uses increasing Coherence to turn several life-stage Echoes into one recognizable Keeper. Other encounters may use it differently.

## Resonance Type Paths

Resonance describes the **true information that activated the Source**.

The danger comes from reality treating that genuine but incomplete truth as sufficient.

| Resonance Type | Restoration Path | What Recognized the Weight | What the Party Learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Relationship** | **Relationship** | A surviving personal or emotional connection. | Who mattered to whom, and what did that bond actually mean? |
| **Pattern** | **Pattern** | A continuing obligation, dependency, ownership chain, routine, institution, or material consequence. | What still exists because of this life? |
| **Choice** | **Choice** | A decision, refusal, coercion, repeated role, or reenactment of agency. | What was actually chosen, resisted, accepted, or imposed? |
| **Form** | **Form** | A likeness, role, representation, expectation, or recognizable outward shape. | What shape is reality being told to reconstruct? |

### Relationship Resonance

Relationship Resonance occurs when a surviving human connection recognizes the Weight carried by a Source.

It may involve family, friendship, partnership, mentorship, care, betrayal, abandonment, grief, dependence, or love.

The connection is genuine.

Gressa and the cloth toy are the simplest example:

> **The toy carries Weight. Gressa recognizes the life through a surviving mother-child relationship. Reality therefore knows “this was her child” before it knows almost anything else about who that child became.**

Restoring Relationship must supply enough truth to make the current interpretation untenable. Merely observing that two people are related is Recognition, not Restoration.

### Pattern Resonance

Pattern Resonance occurs when something a Forgotten life changed continues operating strongly enough to recognize its Weight.

Common sources include:

- property;
- debts and claims;
- care obligations;
- institutional procedures;
- maintenance routines;
- ownership chains;
- recurring work;
- economic or civic dependencies.

A forgotten caretaker's medication schedule can recognize a living Source even when the cared-for person no longer consciously remembers the caretaker.

Pattern asks:

> **What is the world still doing because this person once lived?**

### Choice Resonance

Choice Resonance occurs when a present action, refusal, coercion, role, or reenactment recognizes unresolved Weight through agency.

It is especially useful when history has flattened the difference between what someone chose and what was done to them.

Choice asks:

> **What was actually chosen, resisted, accepted, or imposed?**

A stated preference is not automatically restored **Choice**.

A reconstruction can accurately hear a refusal, desire, or decision and still erase agency if the surrounding people, institution, ritual, or reenactment treats that answer as nonbinding.

For Choice to be truthful, the person's decision must have the causal authority it actually possessed—or the reconstruction must truthfully preserve that the authority was denied.

This distinction is especially important in rescue, containment, treatment, sacrifice, and protection scenarios:

> **Knowing what someone wants is not the same as allowing that choice to govern what happens next.**

### Form Resonance

Form Resonance occurs when a likeness, representation, expectation, role, or socially supplied image gives Weight a recognizable shape.

Portraits, masks, statues, uniforms, stories, Cult artwork, silhouettes, and ritual roles can all provide Form information.

Usually the representation preserves some truth about an underlying person or role. The **Face of the Unknown** demonstrates the important edge case: the truthful information may instead be that **observers really projected this Form**, not that the Form ever belonged to an external being.

A Form can therefore be truthful evidence about the observer while remaining false evidence about the apparent subject.

Form asks:

> **What shape is the present telling reality to reconstruct?**

## Resonance Supplies Truth, Not Completeness

A Resonance condition should almost always be based on something **true**.

Gressa really was the child's mother.

A debt really remained unpaid.

Someone really made a consequential choice.

A portrait may really resemble the person it depicts.

And sometimes the true statement is only:

> **Someone genuinely imagined this Form and supplied it to the Source.**

The Face of the Unknown is the canonical example. Its accumulated representations can be false as descriptions of one external being while remaining truthful records of what generations of observers hoped, feared, desired, and believed they perceived.

The false reconstruction emerges because reality receives a genuine piece of mnemonic evidence without enough Meaning to understand **what that evidence is evidence of**.

> **The problem is not that Resonance lies. The problem is that reality mistakes one recognized truth for a complete person—or mistakes truthful evidence about observers for evidence that an apparent person existed at all.**

That is why breaking Resonance prevents reconstruction without Stabilizing the Weight, and why restoring the Resonant Identity requires more than merely noticing what activated the Source.

Recognition begins the process.

Meaning constrains it.
