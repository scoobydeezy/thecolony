## Opening State

The Party follows the map’s question about whether the Face reveals or instructs. Maelyn welcomes inquiry at Pilgrim’s Rest; Tarn participates if alive and available.

## The Workshop and the Rite

### The Thin Place
Pilgrim's Rest contains generations of devotional art surrounding a smooth, featureless mask.

The space feels more like a communal workshop than a sinister ritual chamber:

- Clay sculptures.
- Charcoal drawings.
- Paintings.
- Carvings.
- Tapestries.
- Silhouettes.
- Amateur work beside extraordinary art.

The works depict mutually contradictory versions of the Unknown.

The Cult does not treat contradiction as error. Its doctrine holds that the Unknown possesses many Forms and that each witness perceives only part of a reality too large to be seen whole.

At the center is the blank mask known as the **Face of the Unknown**.

## Origin and Doctrine

### The Cult's Origin Story
The Cult's history around the Face can be understood as a gradual continuum rather than a single miraculous beginning.

1. **The Blank Face.** A mundane contemplative object teaches that no observer possesses the whole truth.
2. **The Archive.** Pilgrims create artwork after meditation, recording what they believe was revealed to them.
3. **The Feedback Tradition.** Earlier representations shape later expectations. New manifestations resemble old art; new art then records those manifestations.
4. **The Threshold.** After generations of repeated attention, the Face carries enough accumulated Weight for reality to begin bending around it.
5. **The First Manifestations.** Vague silhouettes, changing features, and partial figures begin appearing during rites.
6. **Doctrine of Manifestation.** The Cult concludes that sincere collective reception allows an external Unknown to reveal itself through many partial Forms.
7. **The Mature Rite.** Pilgrims intentionally use selected artworks, contemplation, and group interpretation to invite manifestations.

The Cult therefore has excellent empirical reasons for its belief.

The ritual really did become more powerful over generations.
The manifestations really do respond to what observers perceive.
The art really does predict what appears.

The Cult is wrong about **which direction the information is flowing**.

### What the Cult Believes
The Cult believes:

> **The Unknown already exists beyond perception. The Face allows many witnesses to receive different pieces of its true Form.**

Contradictory images are therefore interpreted as partial revelation rather than projection.

This is why Cult instruction does not warn participants against supplying Form. A pilgrim is told to attend carefully to what reveals itself and to trust that different Forms can each contain part of the truth.

## The Demonstration

Before the Party is asked to take part, Maelyn shows them the rite working. A pilgrim brings an **ordinary object** and uses it as a **second Source**, separate from the Face, together with the accumulated visual impressions of the archive. The manifestation that coheres around the object appears calmly and behaves more like a guest than an attacker. To the Cult this proves that willing reception gives the Unknown permission to reveal itself.

## The Invitation

Offer each participant an artwork and ask what stands out or what it reveals. Preserve their actual words. The contradictory gallery is visible evidence, not a secret deduction locked behind a roll. Participation is voluntary; exceptional observation can lead directly into the benign mastery path described in the encounter.

## GM Truth

The mask began as a mundane contemplative object. Generations of attention supplied real mnemonic Weight. The projecting was real even though the supposed external Someone was not established. Let the Party discover this through cause and effect in the rite instead of having Maelyn announce the solution.
