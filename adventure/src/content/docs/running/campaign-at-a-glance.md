## The Premise

Darkwing is sent to investigate what looks like a straightforward Aspis problem. The expectation is simple: find the bad guys, stop the bad guys, move on.

Instead, the Party finds a prosperous Colony built on an ancient compromise. There is no obvious villain. There is only a system, and every attempt to improve it creates new consequences.

What begins as a mystery becomes a question about responsibility, inherited burdens, unintended consequences, and the cost of certainty. Aspis turns out to be the first institution able to prove that something is missing.

> **The town is not the mystery. The people investigating it are.**

## What Kind of Campaign This Is

*Results Pending* does not hide a correct answer for the players to find. It presents an impossible problem and asks them to build an answer together. Every meaningful decision balances competing values; the ending is not waiting to be uncovered, but emerges from the players' own priorities and relationships.

> **The GM creates conditions. The players create meaning.**

Your job is not to provide the answer. It is to keep the Colony moving, keep its people truthful to what they believe, and let consequences continue.

The campaign's themes: certainty, responsibility, memory, institutional compromise, inherited moral debt, unintended consequences, and hope in the face of complexity. It should encourage humility over certainty, action over paralysis, curiosity over cynicism, questions over easy answers, and hope over despair.

## The Colony

The Colony is not merely the setting. It is the campaign's main character: an independent city-state whose every institution, tradition and faction is a different response to the same truth. No faction exists to be defeated; each believes it is protecting something valuable.

> **Every faction is correct about something. Every faction is blind to something.**

It should never feel like a puzzle waiting to be solved. It should feel like a living system that keeps responding to what the players do. See Part II for the place, its traditions and its factions.

## The Three Acts

| Act | Title | Feels like | Central question | What can't be undone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | The Secret | Conspiracy mystery, folk horror | What is the Colony doing to its Keepers? | The Party decides whether Vara's Reading completes |
| **II** | The Cost | Open investigation, political thriller | What has the Reading left the living responsible for? | Faction methods move from investigation into implementation, and collide |
| **III** | The Reckoning | Supernatural catastrophe, civic siege | Where will the burden go? | The Party reconstructs Aletheia and enacts a new or transformed answer |

**The Reading is the campaign's only required persistent branch.** At the end of Act I the Party decides whether to interrupt the newly Called Keeper's Reading or let her informed choice stand. Everything after reads differently on each path (*If the Reading is Stopped*, *If the Reading Succeeds*); see The Reading and Its Branches. The two paths meet again, mechanically, at Totality.

Everything else is choice within one Colony state. In Act II, for example, the Manor offers five investigations the Party takes in whatever order its choices and the factions' movements suggest; they are opportunities, not paths.

## The Shape of the Ending

Both paths reopen the same deeper problem from opposite directions. Under Totality, the burden can no longer stay inside the old, closed chain of Keepers; memory spreads through people, objects, places, institutions, records and relationships. The Party must first learn to reconstruct forgotten people truthfully. Then it must answer the question the Colony has avoided for generations:

> **Where should the weight rest now?**

The campaign does not contain a predetermined answer. Beneath that question is the one the whole Colony has never answered:

> **Can a society bear the weight of memory together? Or will it always choose someone else to carry it?**
