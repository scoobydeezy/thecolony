## The Act in Brief

The Party follows Aspis's impossible accounting into a Colony preparing to celebrate a newly Called Keeper. Darkwing meets :ref[Vara]{#npc.vara-cairn} as a person before it understands her office, and the choices it makes around her decide whether she comes to believe these strangers respect her judgment.

The supernatural mystery and the faction investigation matter: the Party meets its first Veiled manifestation, watches the Order save lives competently, and tests competing explanations for what happened. But those events are also the circumstances in which Vara learns what kind of people the Party are.

The act's climax is not a fight against the Order. It is the moment the Party asks Vara to trust its judgment over the institution, the history and the personal commitment telling her that the Reading must go on. The act ends with a real choice, made on incomplete information.

## The Act Question

> **What is the Colony really doing to its Keepers?**

## The Relational Question

> **Does Vara have reason to believe Darkwing respects her judgment when it matters?**

## The Apparent Answer

> **It celebrates them, asks them to sacrifice themselves, and then forgets them.**

This is true, and incomplete.

## GM Truths for this Act

:::secret
- **Aspis is not the mystery.** It is documenting absences no one else has treated as a systems problem.
- **Vara's willingness may be sincere.** If she continues the Reading, it is because the lives she encounters become real to her, not because anyone compels her.
- **The Order is competent.** It protects lives well before its secrecy becomes intolerable. Play it that way.
- **The Veiled do not come from the Duskwood.** The official story is a real danger used to explain a different one.
:::

## Vara's Trust

Vara's relationship with the Party is the emotional spine of the act. Three direct opportunities set whether she personally trusts the Party; a fourth, when she is absent, records whether its respect for her agency is real.

| | When | What earns it |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust 1 — Inclusion** | At the :ref[House of Abiding]{#event.the-house-of-abiding}, she asks to come along to check the bad map | Saying yes. Refusing **locks her Trust at 0 for Act I**: later kindness can matter emotionally, but she will not stake the Colony on Darkwing's judgment |
| **Trust 2 — Confidence** | While the Party is held by the Order (:ref[Prisoners]{#event.prisoners}), she shows them her professional cartographer's compass, which she believes she has simply always owned; later Serevan asks about unexplained possessions | Recognizing the compass may qualify, and deliberately keeping her confidence. Missing the connection earns nothing but is not a betrayal |
| **Trust 3 — Continued participation** | Before the Session 3 investigation, she asks to come again | Letting her come, now that everyone knows mnemonic phenomena can kill. Refusing earns nothing; existing Trust remains |
| **Agency Stance** (no Trust) | Dawn's conversation in :ref[An Opportunity]{#event.an-opportunity}, with Vara away preparing | Recorded as agency-supportive, procedural or agency-denying; it modifies the Appeal in :ref[The Reading]{#encounter.the-reading} |

How the Party talks about Vara when she is not in the room shows whether its professed respect is real.

## Routes Through the Act

The act opens in sequence (The Calling → The House of Abiding → Bad Maps → Prisoners).

**Session 3** investigates the first manifestation through the route the current Source state and Darkwing's choices produce. Every route undermines the official Duskwood explanation in a different way.

| Route | Available when |
|---|---|
| :ref[A Life]{#encounter.a-life} (Witnesses) | The cloth toy remains the intact Source |
| :ref[Containment]{#encounter.containment} (EGRESS) | The toy Ruptured, and its Weight passed into Gressa |
| :ref[Duskwood]{#encounter.duskwood} (Cult) | Always |

**The convergence** (:ref[An Opportunity]{#event.an-opportunity}): Miriel Stoutfoot and Julian Arrett of Dawn offer access to the procession and the Ritual Chamber; Dawn will protest along the procession line as a distraction. Serevan knows, and asks the Party to help remove the protesters instead, to avoid a political scene. Both sides promise access to the Ritual Chamber. **The Party must choose a side.** The act closes with the Reading.

## What Changes by the End

By the end of Act I, the Party should:

- know Vara as a person before understanding the ritual;
- have made consequential choices about including her, keeping her confidence, and letting her take risks concerning her own future;
- have shown, in the Dawn conversation, whether it treats her agency as intrinsic or conditional on her choosing what Darkwing prefers;
- have met one contained Veiled manifestation, and learned that Fractured Objects can preserve dangerous relationships;
- have seen the Order protect lives competently, and gained reason to doubt the Duskwood explanation;
- suspect that previous Keepers are being removed from collective memory;
- understand that Vara's willingness to continue the Reading may be sincere;
- reach the Ritual Chamber with enough evidence to make either intervention or restraint compassionate and credible.

## Suggested Pacing

Sessions are pacing, not chapter boundaries. The act's emotional progression runs **curiosity → relationship → confidence → agency → urgency**.

| Session | Items |
|---|---|
| 1 | The Calling, The House of Abiding |
| 2 | Bad Maps, Prisoners |
| 3 | One of A Life, Containment or Duskwood; An Opportunity |
| 4 | The Reading |
