## An Investigative Resource

## Pattern Without Another Major Route
The Clearing House is circled on the Observer's map but should not become a sixth full encounter.

Perrik and the Claims Registry function as Act II's **cross-reference engine**.

The Party can revisit them between substantial routes and bring whatever it has recovered.

The Claims Registry traces the material wake left by a life:

- Property.
- Debt.
- Dependents.
- Payments.
- Maintenance.
- Inheritance.
- Contracts.
- Civic obligations.
- Supply chains.

Examples:

- Broken Bell evidence can expose Old Town ownership discontinuities.
- An EGRESS subject number can be connected to a vanished household.
- Dawn excavation evidence can reveal historical purchases or false property claims.
- A Veiled House object can be traced through taxes, repairs, or transfers after its owner was forgotten.

The lesson is:

> **Forgetting a person does not erase what their life changed.**

The Party does not need to own the Claims Registry.

Reliable access to Perrik and the Clearing House can be enough to make Pattern available later.

## Using Perrik’s Time

Bring a specific record, object, obligation or absence. Perrik traces a material connection and explains its limits; he does not conjure an absent Name from accounting. Useful corroboration can be obtained without ownership of the Claims Registry. Record any bargain, personal trust or access restriction established here for his finale contribution.

The local administrator cares about the Colony’s functioning. The wider Consortium also sees commercial value in contracts that remain reliable here. Keep those motives distinct when negotiating.
