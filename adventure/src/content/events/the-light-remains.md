## Opening State

The Party, Aletheia and the participating people have agreed on a viable way to carry the Weight. Keep every established loss, refusal and compromise.

## The Final Reading

This enacts the answer already given. **No new test, surprise failure or last-minute price is introduced.**

Once the Party and Aletheia have reached a viable answer to the Anchoring problem, move directly into its implementation.

The exact form depends entirely upon what the table created.

It may involve:
* A transformed relationship with the Book of Names.
* One Keeper or several voluntary bearers.
* Memory divided among people, places, objects, and institutions.
* The Face of the Unknown helping preserve truthful Form.
* EGRESS measuring capacity or routing portions of the burden.
* Witnesses preserving names, relationships, and context.
* Aspis tracing obligations and material consequences.
* Order protections containing dangerous transfers without controlling their meaning.
* Dawn establishing consent, public legitimacy, or mechanisms for revision.
* Cult practices allowing willing reception and relationship.
* Shattered evidence ensuring hidden lives cannot simply disappear again.
* Old Town becoming an active civic memory rather than a buried wound.
* A solution no faction could have designed independently.

Do not require the Party to repeat the negotiations that made these things available.

A faction that agreed to participate does so.

A faction that refused remains absent or pursues whatever limited role was established.

An Asset that was destroyed remains unavailable.

A compromise made during **Aletheia** remains part of the system rather than becoming a new obstacle.

The GM's job is to translate the Party's answer into images.

## The Final Passage
The implementation may deliberately echo the old ritual axis:

**House of Abiding → Founder’s Way → Monument of the First Keeper → Memorial Building → Ritual Chamber**

But it does not need to reproduce the Reading.
The point of the echo is contrast.

Where one Keeper once walked alone, there may now be many people.
Where citizens once watched, they may now participate.

The Book may leave the Chamber.
Names may be spoken publicly.

Objects may return to homes instead of archives.
Engrams may be distributed instead of sealed.

The Monument may become a place where Aletheia is finally named.

Or the Party may reject the ritual axis entirely.
Follow the answer the players actually built.

## What Will You Carry?

Before the transfer resolves, return authorship to the players one final time.

Throughout the campaign, Veiled encounters have asked the table to help reconstruct forgotten people from incomplete evidence.

The final prompt turns that same principle toward the living.

Ask each player:
**What will your character carry?**

Do not define *carry* too narrowly.

The answer may be literal mnemonic weight if Darkwing's system uses voluntary living Anchors.

It may instead be:
* A specific person's memory.
* A relationship.
* A name.
* An obligation.
* A piece of the Colony's history.
* Responsibility for an institution.
* Grief they previously wanted removed.
* A promise to Vara.
* Something learned from Aletheia.
* Responsibility for a mistake Darkwing made.
* A question they refuse to let become doctrine.
* Something their character intends to teach the people who come after them.

The player decides.

Their answer becomes canon.

If compatible with the system the Party constructed, it may also become part of the metaphysical transfer itself.

The campaign began with one person accepting responsibility for everyone else.

It ends by asking each player what responsibility their character is willing to accept **without assuming that everyone must accept the same thing**.

## The Return of Light
When the accumulated weight finds viable destinations, the eclipse moves.

Do not call for a final check.
Do not make the return of sunlight contingent upon one last die roll.

The table has already done the work necessary to reach this moment.
A thin line of sunlight appears across the Colony.

Its path should reflect the answer Darkwing built.

It might cross:
* Vara.
* Aletheia.
* The Book opened beneath the sky.
* Several voluntary carriers standing apart from one another.
* Old Tom among the graves of Old Town.
* The Face of the Unknown, finally blank again.
* The Engram Vault as its load begins to fall.
* The Claims Registry surrounded by names that finally have people attached to them.
* Uldren and Celia standing together.
* Founder’s Way filled with citizens rather than spectators.
* The Monument where the Colony finally knows the First Keeper's name.

Then the shadow begins to move.

Totality ends.

The light does not return because the Party discovered the perfect answer. 

It returns because the weight has somewhere to rest.

## Aletheia and the Forgotten
The fate of Aletheia and the other Forgotten follows from the system the Party created.

They may:
* Remain consciously remembered.
* Become accessible through particular relationships or Anchors.
* Return to the Book under different conditions.
* Become part of public history without remaining continuously perceptible.
* Rest beyond ordinary conscious access while their names and consequences remain acknowledged.
* Occupy some new relationship with the Colony that no previous institution permitted.

Do not assume that maximum remembrance is automatically the best outcome.

Remembering a person does not create ownership over them.

If Aletheia or another Forgotten chooses to recede, that choice can itself be part of the answer.

## The Colony Afterward
Move outward from the supernatural resolution into ordinary life.

Show what has changed.

Answer only the questions made relevant by play.

Possible consequences include:
* What becomes of Vara?
* What relationship, if any, survives between Vara and Marek?
* What becomes of Aletheia?
* What becomes of the Book of Names?
* Does the Order of Theia retain its name after learning what **Theia** meant?
* What happens to the Monument of the First Keeper?
* Who administers whatever system now carries mnemonic weight?
* Which factions endure, merge, fracture, reform, or disappear?
* What becomes of Old Town and Sundara's recovered history?
* What happens to EGRESS and the Engram Vault?
* What role does the Claims Registry now play?
* What do Uldren and Celia do when the institutional sequence that shaped their marriage changes?
* How does Miriel understand Dawn after recovering the revolution it forgot?
* What becomes of the Cult's Face and its archive of Pilgrims?
* Does Pilgrim's Rest remain a place whose guests can freely choose to depart, and what has Maelyn learned?
* Did the Counterfeit Weaver finish her new magical object, and did anyone willingly take it beyond the Hall?
* What happens to the Shattered once concealed history is no longer quite so easy to conceal?
* Which of the Colony's negative-space traditions survive?
* Which traditions change now that people understand the absence beneath them?

Do not resolve every institution into harmony.

The Colony should still contain disagreement.

The Party has created an answer, not ended politics.

## The Negative Spaces
Use the Colony's ordinary traditions as quiet evidence that its relationship with absence has changed.

The Open Place may still remain empty—but now people know why they leave room.
The Second Portion may still be given away—but as a conscious act of responsibility rather than an unexplained habit.
An Unfinished Likeness may deliberately preserve room for people absent from the moment without pretending no one was lost.
The Spare Hand may become one principle underlying a distributed carrying system:
**No necessary burden should ever belong to only one person.**
The Resting Stroke may still remain silent.

But perhaps, for the first time, someone can explain what the silence is for.
Not every custom needs to change.
The important difference is that the Colony can finally remember the **question** beneath the tradition.

## The Meaning of the Phrase
Someone says:
**The light remains.**

Do not explain what the phrase means now.

Let the campaign determine that through context.
It may mean:
* The forgotten still matter.
* No one carries everything alone.
* Absence does not erase consequence.
* Memory can rest without disappearing.
* Hope survives uncertainty.
* An answer can change without becoming failure.
* Responsibility belongs to the living precisely because the dead cannot carry everything forever.

Its meaning belongs to this version of the Colony.

## The Observer
The Observer does not evaluate Darkwing's solution.

He does not announce whether it will work for fifty years.

He does not identify the flaw the players failed to notice.

For once, he has no useful question that the Party has not already asked itself.

His final entry remains:
**Settlement 58.**
**Results pending.**

Whether the Party has disproved his certainty is less important than the fact that, for the first time in many years, he cannot write the conclusion.

## Answer
**The burden cannot be escaped. It can only be carried. The Party decided what it was willing to carry, what it could ask others to carry, and what must never again be assigned simply because someone was capable of bearing it.**

## Final Question Preserved
The campaign ends with an answer.

It should also preserve the question that answer was built to address:
**When circumstances change, will the Colony remember to change its answer?**

The light remains.

## Possible Next Developments

Close with ordinary consequences made relevant by this table’s choices. The Observer’s Results pending is an open future, not a verdict or a sequel threat that invalidates the ending.
