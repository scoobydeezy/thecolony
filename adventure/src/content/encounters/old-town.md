## At the Table

**Objective:** recognize and Name all six distinct lives. **Board:** one permanent Shell per PC (normally six), each with Head, Arms and Back fragments; Recognition Locks belong to configurations. **Track:** which lives are Named, current fragments, and Dispersal returns. **Limit:** this resolves the acute Chorus; Sundara’s grave, whistle and Name remain for Sunny.

## Mechanical Reference

Use :ref[Old Town Corpus and fragment packages]{#creature.old-town-corpus}. The six historical personal names printed there are working labels, never required answers.

### Narrative Container
Old Town was never truly resolved. During the original catastrophe, **many grave-related Sources** generated an outbreak of zombie-shaped Veiled; the Colony deliberately burned the district and destroyed Source vessels to stop it, causing Rupture, Scars, and the enduring environmental Wound. This history should be learned gradually rather than recited to players. Tomlin Cobb remains there tending the dead. Over decades he became the **dominant living Recognition focus** of its quieted manifestation, though not the original or only Source of its distributed Weight. His fractured Relationship with Sundara keeps collapsing many remembered personal Names toward **Sunny**; **this Act II encounter does not reveal Sundara's grave, marker, whistle, or true Name**.

Darkwing arrives expecting to investigate a historical crime scene. Instead, asking who “Sunny” is brings the unresolved cemetery problem back into active reconstruction.

### Opening State
- Old Town is abandoned behind a newer section of Colony wall constructed after the catastrophe.
- The cemetery is a **distributed Source field** made from many unresolved graves rather than one portable object.
- Tom remembers faces, relationships, places, routines, deaths, and belongings with unusual precision, but cannot maintain stable personal Names.
- The cemetery is metastable rather than dormant: many broken identities repeatedly collapse toward Tom's surviving Relationship with Sundara instead of freely propagating through Old Town.
- The cemetery is full of ordinary graves, including damaged, illegible, and half-buried markers. **There are not six conspicuously important headstones to match.** Successful Recognition finds the relevant grave among the whole cemetery. Its personal name is illegible, although a motif, epitaph fragment, or Tom's relational recollection may become legible.
- The six life kernels, grave motifs, and occupational truths in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page are fixed GM evidence; the historical personal-name labels in that reference are **working labels, not names the players must discover**. Players supply each life's Name after truthfully recognizing it.

### Encounter Flow
1. **The Shattered send Darkwing to look rather than believe.** Physical evidence in Old Town undermines the official account of a Veiled attack arriving from the Duskwood.
2. **Tom leads them into the cemetery.** His universal “Sunny” initially reads as eccentricity. His otherwise precise memory makes the missing Names conspicuous.
3. **The field wakes.** During Tom's ordinary circuit of the cemetery, his attempt to identify whom he means by “Sunny” makes the unresolved Recognition surge. **Do not single out, locate, inspect, or reveal Sundara's gravestone in this encounter.** Tom tends many damaged markers, and the Party cannot distinguish her burial plot yet.
4. **A Chorus manifests.** Create one **Corpus per player present**, normally six. Each Corpus has a permanent Shell and three visibly mismatched, migratory Fragments: Head, Arms, Back.
5. **The Shell tells on itself.** Immediately before taking its three actions on each turn, every manifested Shell repeats its one fixed, distinctive bodily tell. The motion never changes when the pieces swap, but its meaning may be almost unreadable until some rightful pieces return. Describe the physical motion, not its interpretation; players are free to try to read it, but do not force a game of charades.
6. **Dispersal redistributes rather than solves.** When a Corpus is Dispersed, its Fragments swap among the remaining unnamed Shells and it later reconstructs. Combat capabilities can move between bodies. A changed configuration can also make a previously failed identification possible again.
7. **Recognize a Life is the investigation action.** A PC spends **two actions** choosing one observed, unnamed, currently manifested and unlocked Shell, proposes a relevant Recall Knowledge skill, and attempts a **secret** check. Each Shell has two excellent, two reasonable, and two stretch skills; unrelated skills are ineligible. The DC also increases for every incorrect piece currently attached to that Shell. Exact skill tiers, DCs, and outcomes are in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page.
8. **One failure locks one configuration.** On a failure, place a small **Recognition Lock token beneath that Shell**. Nobody can try again against that Shell until at least one of its actual Head, Arms, or Back pieces changes. Clear the token on a real swap, including one caused by a successful Naming elsewhere. A critical failure supplies a plausible but **false reading of the gesture** and immediately empowers the mistaken Corpus; it does not author a false canonical Name.
9. **Success discovers a connection, not a written answer.** On a successful check, the PC recognizes the real role or meaning behind the Shell's repeated motion and notices or locates its associated grave among the cemetery's many markers. Tom can confirm relationships and circumstances, but **no legible inscription supplies a personal name**.
10. **The players supply a Name.** Ask, **“Who were they?”** The player or party chooses the individual's Name (and may add a fitting small detail). The Name is now canon for that life. No second check or extra action is required. The correct Head, Arms, and Back return to that Shell, misplaced parts swap back by the existing conservation rule, and the life becomes a permanent **Named Boundary**.
11. **The fight resolves all six lives.** Each Named Boundary removes that Corpus from combat and from further fragment migration. No arbitrary three- or four-Name cutoff: the remaining pool becomes increasingly legible as its wrong pieces are eliminated. After five Names, all surviving fragments belong to the final Shell; it can still be Named using the same procedure (its configuration is fully correct). Clear any Recognition Lock on that last Shell when it becomes the sole remaining life, so the encounter cannot deadlock.
12. **The missing Sunny remains.** As the six lives find their own Named Boundaries, Tom becomes clearer, but his repeated “Sunny” persists. Darkwing may suspect the fragment points to one unresolved person, yet **neither her grave, any surviving inscription, nor her full Name is located or revealed here**. The damaged **SUNDA—** marker, its associated Fractured Object, and final Name recovery belong to **Sunny**. Sundara's Name is fixed GM truth, not one of the six player-authored Corpus Names.

### Player Leverage
Darkwing can:
- Disperse Corpora for temporary safety and to force fragment swaps that change both combat abilities and Recognition difficulty.
- Watch the fixed pre-action Shell tells and choose skills that fit the observed motion. **Head/Arms/Back show context; the Shell is the persistent individual.**
- Attempt **Recognize a Life ◆◆** when a Shell's current pieces and an available skill make the check attractive.
- Use Tom's memories and the graveyard's ambient evidence to understand successful Recognition; no six-grave search grid, clue-counter, or player biography quiz is required.
- After a successful check, author the personal Name and create a permanent Named Boundary; the players never need to manually gather the correct three pieces before Naming.

The tell is always a concrete repeated movement, not the personality, ancestry, or abilities of whatever pieces happen to be attached. Its ambiguity becomes understandable through **skill choice, the current three fragments, and one roll**, not through mandatory pantomime interpretation.

### Restoration / Truth Condition
For each Corpus, the underlying truth is:

> **This recurring behavior belongs to this particular life, represented by this grave, not to whichever borrowed fragments it currently wears.**

A successful Recognition establishes that association. The players then answer **“Who were they?”** and supply the Name that fixes this individual's boundary. The GM controls the fixed life evidence and fragment ownership; the players control the previously unrecoverable personal name and may add compatible biographical detail. The six old working names in the GM mechanical reference are placeholders, not solution strings.

### Resolution
When **all six** Corpora are Named, the acute Chorus ends. Each restored individual settles into their proper grave as an Anchor, and the cemetery's fragment interchange stops.

This resolves the **acute Incursion**, not Old Town.

The larger spill remains unresolved. Tom remains its dominant false attractor. The district is still dangerous and still geographically contained rather than healed. **Do not reveal Sundara's particular gravestone or its associated object as a closing clue.** The six previously identified and Anchored graves remain valid, known places and can reward prior investigation in Sunny.

Darkwing leaves knowing both that the official Duskwood story is false and that Old Town's catastrophe is still, in an important sense, happening.

### Systems Established
**Chorus, Corpora, repeatable Shell tells, fragment-dependent Recognition DC, skill-relevance tiers, configuration-based Recognition Lock, player-authored Names, Named Boundaries, graves as restored Anchors, distributed Source fields, Resonance cascade, partial relief without full Resolution.**

The complete action, six tell scripts, skill matrix, DCs, false-Recognition consequences, fragment-slot abilities, and randomized swap procedure belong in *the mechanical reference entries linked on this page*.

## Recognition and Fragment Procedure

### Recognize a Life ◆◆
**CONCENTRATE, SECRET** — Encounter-specific two-action Recall Knowledge activity.

**Requirements:** Choose one **currently manifested, unnamed Corpus** whose fixed Shell tell you have observed. It is not **Recognition Locked**. A Corpus can be targeted even if its current head, arms, or back belongs to someone else. The Grave field is accessible as a whole: the PC does not need to stand beside, select, or individually search a particular marker.

**Procedure:** The character says what sort of action or practice they are trying to understand and selects one plausible skill. The GM makes a **secret Recall Knowledge check** against the tier DC below, modified by the current mismatch. A player is not required to solve a charade or guess a specific occupation to declare the activity. The skill's relevance represents their interpretive approach. Use only the six explicitly eligible skills for that Shell from the table; the other skills do not work. A listed Lore can be attempted as normal if the character possesses it. If a player proposes an ineligible skill, explain that this approach cannot identify the Shell **before** they commit the activity; do not roll against a secret impossible DC.

**Recognition DC = skill-tier base DC + 3 per incorrect Fragment currently attached to the selected Shell.** Compare each of its actual Head, Arms, and Back to that Shell's true set. Do not count its fixed Shell itself, and do not count a Fragment's visual oddness as additional mismatch.

| Skill tier | Base DC | 0 wrong | 1 wrong | 2 wrong | 3 wrong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Excellent** | 24 | 24 | 27 | 30 | 33 |
| **Reasonable** | 30 | 30 | 33 | 36 | 39 |
| **Stretch** | 36 | 36 | 39 | 42 | 45 |
| **Unrelated** | — | Not eligible | Not eligible | Not eligible | Not eligible |

These DC tiers are a deliberate encounter-specific adjustment for a typical six-PC level-12 party. The creature level remains 10. Use the character's actual skill modifier and ordinary PF2e critical success/failure rules; the table measures *relevance*, not training or proficiency.

| Shell (GM working label) | Two Excellent skills | Two Reasonable skills | Two Stretch skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Rescuer** | Nature, River Lore | Survival, Medicine | Society, Crafting |
| **2 — Mason** | Crafting, Engineering Lore | Society, Athletics¹ | Arcana, Nature |
| **3 — Apothecary** | Medicine, Herbalism Lore | Nature, Crafting | Religion, Occultism |
| **4 — Courier** | Survival, Courier Lore | Society, Nature | Crafting, Arcana |
| **5 — Defender** | Society, Warfare Lore | Athletics¹, Medicine | Religion, Survival |
| **6 — Cartographer** | Occultism, Cartography Lore | Arcana, Crafting | Nature, Religion |

¹ **Athletics used as a knowledge check** describes understanding trained posture, weight transfer, and bodily technique, not physically overpowering a Shell. For this special Recall Knowledge use, roll with **Intelligence + Athletics proficiency** rather than Strength + Athletics proficiency. This skill is eligible only for the listed Shells and tiers.

**Critical Success:** Connect the Shell's stable tell to a particular grave and its genuine work, responsibility, or lived practice. Reveal the grave's motif and surviving epitaph and provide an additional illuminating detail from Tom or the surroundings. Its personal name is **not legible**. The party may now author that person's Name and form a Named Boundary immediately, with no further action or roll.

**Success:** Connect the Shell to its particular grave and a truthful interpretation of its tell; reveal enough motif/epitaph/relational context to make the correspondence legible. The personal name is **not legible**. The party now authors its Name and forms a Named Boundary immediately, with no further action or roll.

**Failure:** No reliable identification. Put one **Recognition Lock** token beneath the selected Shell. It cannot be the target of another Recognize a Life attempt by **any character** until its actual Head, Arms, or Back configuration changes.

**Critical Failure — False Recognition:** Give one **convincing but incorrect interpretation** of the motion (never an obligatory historical name). The Corpus immediately **regains 20 HP, up to its maximum**, and becomes **quickened 1 until the end of its next turn**; the extra action can be used only to **Stride or Strike**. Put the same Recognition Lock token beneath it. The false reading is not canon, cannot create a Named Boundary, and does not itself swap Fragments.

**Recognition Lock clearing:** Remove the token from each unnamed Shell whose **actual attached Head, Arms, or Back piece changes**, whether through Dispersal redistribution or Named Boundary restoration. A d6 roll that swaps a piece with itself does not change configuration and does not clear the token. The lock belongs to **that Shell's current configuration**, not to one character or one skill. When only one unnamed Corpus remains, clear any outstanding lock: the other five completed Named Boundaries uniquely constrain its remaining identity. A Named Corpus is removed permanently and never uses this token again.

**Graves and authorship:** Do not put six special name cards on the board. A successful check includes locating the right ordinary grave somewhere in the cemetery—no grave-search bookkeeping. Tom remembers relationships and patterns, not Names. Ask **“Who were they?”**; the group supplies the Name and may add one compatible personal detail. Their choice **replaces the GM working label** for that life but never changes its Shell number, profession, fixed fragments, or existing grave evidence. The Name is true because the recognized identity boundary has been established, not because a player guessed a secret string.

**Last life:** All six must be Named. After five Named Boundaries, all surviving interchangeable Fragments belong to the last unnamed Shell and its mismatch modifier is **+0**. Use the same check if Darkwing still needs Recognition; the encounter does not end automatically at four or five Names. If Darkwing has already established the last connection through the other five, applying that truth and authoring its Name needs no redundant roll.

### Fragment Redistribution
When an **unnamed** Corpus Disperses, resolve the following immediately:

1. Roll **1d6 for its Head**. Swap that Head with the Head currently occupying the corresponding numbered Corpus.
2. Roll **1d6 for its Arms**. Swap those Arms with the Arms currently occupying the corresponding numbered Corpus.
3. Roll **1d6 for its Back**. Swap that Back with the Back currently occupying the corresponding numbered Corpus.
4. A roll equal to the Dispersed Corpus's own number causes no swap for that Fragment.
5. A Dispersed but unnamed Corpus remains a valid destination. Fragments can therefore move onto a currently absent Corpus and return with it later.
6. A **Named** Corpus is not a legal destination. Reroll results that point to a Named Corpus.

Each die is rolled independently. A Dispersal can therefore move zero, one, two, or all three Fragments.

The same-type swap rule preserves the total Fragment set exactly:

> **Head swaps with Head. Arms swap with Arms. Back swaps with Back.**

No Fragment enters an abstract off-board pool.

**Recognition Lock interaction:** After the three swaps, remove any Recognition Lock tokens from unnamed Shells whose Head, Arms, or Back **actually changed**. The Dispersed Shell can be Recognition Locked while absent; it remains ineligible to be targeted until it reconstructs, and a real piece change clears its token even during Dispersal.

### Named Boundary
When **Recognize a Life** succeeds, the party has truthfully connected that Shell to its particular grave and the life it represents. The player or party now **authors the personal Name**, because its original inscription is illegible. Speaking or otherwise presenting that authored Name immediately Restores the **Name** path; **there is no second action or skill check**. Existing printed personal names are GM working labels only, never required guess strings. A direct, fully evidenced connection already established through play likewise needs no redundant recognition roll.

Resolve the Named Boundary immediately:

1. Locate that life's true Head, Arms, and Back Fragments wherever they currently are.
2. Return each true Fragment to the Named life's Shell.
3. Each displaced Fragment swaps into the corresponding slot from which the rightful Fragment was recovered, preserving conservation.
4. The correctly assembled Corpus becomes briefly coherent and ceases hostile behavior.
5. The life and all four of its pieces are removed permanently from the active Chorus.
6. Its Corpus number remains reserved but is now **Named and removed**. Future Fragment Redistribution rerolls that number; this permanent Named state is distinct from a temporary **Recognition Lock** token.
7. Clear Recognition Lock tokens from any remaining unnamed Shell whose pieces changed during this restoration. If only one unnamed life remains, clear its lock regardless, because the other five names uniquely constrain the last configuration.

A Named Boundary does not require the assembled true configuration to be tactically weaker than the scrambled one. Some true configurations are intentionally excellent combat packages. The hostility ends because the life has become truthfully bounded, not because understanding has weakened it.

## Preparing the Six Bodies

## True Configurations
These are mechanical fragment-owner labels, **not** player-facing historical Names. When a player authors a Name, keep the row's Shell number and piece assignment unchanged and record the new Name beside that row. The correct assemblies are:

| Corpus | Shell | Head | Arms | Back |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Mara | Mara | Rescue Hook | Lifeline Coil |
| **2** | Dorrik | Dorrik | Mason's Maul | Mason's Frame |
| **3** | Seraith | Seraith | Apothecary Hands | Apothecary Rack |
| **4** | Koru | Koru | Signal Lantern | Courier Rig |
| **5** | Ketha | Ketha | Defender's Spear | Broad Shield |
| **6** | Szik | Szik | Star-Staff | Mnemonic Lattice |

These true configurations are **not** intended to be uniformly weak. Ketha's Spear + Shield and Szik's Star-Staff + Lattice are deliberately strong coherent combinations.

## Starting Scramble
Do not begin the encounter with correctly assembled lives.

Before play, assign the eighteen migratory Fragments so that:

* every Corpus begins with at least one visibly incorrect Fragment;
* no Corpus begins fully correct;
* every life retains its fixed Shell tell and its associated grave evidence, even if the tell initially looks misleading;
* the Star-Staff is immediately visible somewhere on the battlefield;
* at least one opening Arms + Back combination is tactically dangerous;
* at least one opening combination is visually or tactically absurd.

The **opening scramble may be curated** for readability and first-round drama.

After the first Corpus Disperses, do not curate subsequent migrations. Resolve Fragment Redistribution by the dice and allow the Chorus to produce whatever combinations result.

## Deliberately Uneven Combinations
Examples of desirable emergent configurations include:

* **Star-Staff + Courier Rig** — mobile artillery that can use a two-action burst and immediately Step.
* **Star-Staff + Broad Shield** — difficult-to-remove ranged threat that also protects neighboring Corpora.
* **Signal Lantern + Mnemonic Lattice** — amplified 30-foot control cone.
* **Defender's Spear + Broad Shield** — exceptionally coherent defensive package; also Ketha's true configuration.
* **Rescue Hook + Mason's Frame** — nearly immovable controller that drags other creatures toward itself.
* **Apothecary Hands + Lifeline Coil** — an inappropriate caretaker that pulls creatures closer in order to subject them to remembered treatment.
* **Mason's Maul + Courier Rig** — highly mobile heavy melee package with terrain disruption.
* **Apothecary Hands + Mason's Frame** — durable but slow debuffer whose defensive rig works against its need to reach patients.

Do not treat silly configurations as mechanical failures. They are evidence that genuine Fragments are being assembled without a stable Name boundary.

## Readability Principle
At any moment, a Corpus should be readable as four questions:

> **Shell — Which underlying life am I tracking?**  
> **Head — Whose visible identity is incorrectly attached here?**  
> **Arms — What can this body actively do right now?**  
> **Back — What secondary trait, defense, mobility, or reaction does it currently possess?**

A player should be able to watch a Corpus Disperse, see a Fragment physically change bodies, and immediately understand why the next creature now possesses that capability.

## If the Party Leaves

Keep every Named Boundary already earned. Do not erase Names to reset the board. Tom remains at the cemetery; the surviving unresolved field can quiet again when the immediate confrontation ends, without resolving Old Town. The later Sunny encounter remains available.
