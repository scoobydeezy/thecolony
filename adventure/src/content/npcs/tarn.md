## Overview

The Roadwarden is an orc warrior responsible for maintaining the route to the Duskwood Shrine and protecting those who choose to travel it.
They escort pilgrims, recover the lost or injured, confront mundane threats, and distinguish physical dangers from the mnemonic phenomena the Cult seeks to understand.

## Personality

Calm, literal, and emotionally direct. They do not speak in riddles or mistake every unusual event for revelation.
When tracks belong to wolves, they say so. When a patrol fled from fear rather than pursuit, they point out the absence of evidence. Their grounded judgment makes them the Cult’s most reliable guide beyond the Colony.

## Motivation

Bring willing seekers safely toward the unknown and accept responsibility for bringing them home again.

## Primary Protection

**Those who choose to approach the unknown.**
They believe voluntary risk creates responsibility for the Cult as well as the pilgrim. A person may choose danger, but that does not absolve their guides from preparation, protection, or rescue.

## Fears

That someone will freely choose danger under their protection and never return because willingness was mistaken for readiness.

## Blind Spot

They believe freely accepted risk becomes morally permissible.
They take informed consent seriously, but may overlook pressures created by faith, desperation, loyalty, or the desire to prove oneself. Once someone clearly agrees, they can tolerate consequences others would consider unacceptable.

## Narrative Role

The Roadwarden grounds the Cult’s mystical interpretation in practical experience.
They demonstrate that emotional openness does not require abandoning caution, while exposing the limits of consent when willingness exceeds capacity. Their journey through the Duskwood helps prove that its dangers are material, traceable, and fundamentally unlike the Veiled.

## Portrayal Notes

Calm, literal, and grounded. Never mystify a physical danger that can be named plainly. Their affection and loyalty appear through position, preparation, and repair rather than confession.

## Pressure Response

Their language becomes shorter and entirely literal. They physically place themselves between danger and the person who chose to approach it.

## Signature Quote

*“An open door still needs someone watching it.”*

## Signature Object

A plain, unnamed, meticulously maintained sword.

## Performance Inspiration

Dave Bautista in Blade Runner

## The Sword

They carry a plain, well-maintained sword with no name, decoration, or inherited history.
Their former weapon was a treasured heirloom. In accordance with Cult practice, they surrendered it to Pilgrim’s Rest, where it now waits in one of the prepared rooms.
They do not treat the current sword as an extension of identity. It is a tool for bringing people home.

## Relationship to the Party

The Roadwarden accompanies Darkwing to the Duskwood Shrine and serves as the expedition’s local authority.
They are neither impressed nor intimidated by the Party’s reputation. Their respect depends on whether Darkwing listens, protects others, and distinguishes courage from unnecessary risk.
Their competence helps establish that the Duskwood contains genuine physical threats—but not the Veiled.

## Relationship to the Host

They admire the Host’s capacity to remain welcoming despite everything she has witnessed.
They repair Pilgrim’s Rest without being asked, challenge her more directly than other adherents, and position themselves between her and danger without drawing attention to it.
They refuse to treat duty, proximity, or affection as a claim upon her. Their respect for her freedom also gives them a reason never to speak.
