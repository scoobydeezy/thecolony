## Overview

His Prominence is the leader of the Order of Theia and one of the few people in the Colony who understands the full cost and function of the Reading.
He knows the Keeper will be forgotten. He knows the sacrifice is concealed. He also knows the Ritual genuinely prevents catastrophic mnemonic collapse.
To him, allowing it to fail is unthinkable.

## Personality

Controlled, formal, compassionate, and absolute. He rarely raises his voice, explains himself defensively, or acknowledges uncertainty in public.
He does not present the Reading as a belief or proposal. He presents it as reality.
His authority conceals a profound fear: that one moment of hesitation will doom the Colony he was entrusted to preserve.

## Motivation

Preserve the Reading and the systems surrounding it until an alternative has already been proven capable of protecting the Colony.

## Primary Protection

**The continuity of the Colony.**
He protects not only the people currently living within it, but the civilization, institutions, and future generations that must survive after them.

## Fears

That one preventable moment of hesitation will cause a catastrophic collapse and name thousands in the Keeper’s place.

## Blind Spot

He believes necessity grants authority.
Because someone must decide, he assumes the person who understands the danger most fully has the right to impose the answer. He has confused accepting responsibility for a sacrifice with possessing the authority to assign it.

## Narrative Role

His Prominence embodies the strongest possible defense of the inherited answer: a compassionate authority who understands that the Reading is unjust and knows that it genuinely works.
He is not defending a lie. He is defending an incomplete answer, with full knowledge of its injustice and complete certainty that the alternative is catastrophe.
If the Reading is interrupted, he is the final obstacle between the Party and the Chamber, and later represents the Order's attempt to restore its authority. His defeat removes one of the only people who understood what the Party has stopped, fractures the Order's leadership, and forces the Colony to confront the consequences without its most prepared defender.
If the Reading succeeds, he recognizes the Party's reconstruction of the Keeper as a dangerous second anchor and orders the emergency Closure intended to preserve the completed transfer.

## Portrayal Notes

Restrained, compassionate, faintly weary, and completely unyielding once necessity has been established. He should sound like a kind man who has already mourned the decision he is about to impose. He does not intimidate through volume; he speaks as though the terrible conclusion was settled long before anyone entered the room.

## Pressure Response

As pressure increases, he becomes gentler, quieter, and more personal while his willingness to compromise disappears. Anger would suggest uncertainty; sorrow confirms that he has already accepted the cost.

## Signature Quote

*“You believe one innocent life is too high a price. I believe you are about to name thousands in her place.”*

## Signature Object

A plain iron key to the Ritual Chamber, worn beneath his formal robes.

## Performance Inspiration

Bill Nighy

## Notes

He is approximately 260 years old -- old enough to remember the Order before its present political dominance, but born after Aletheia’s disappearance and without a personal relationship to her.

## Relationship to the Ritual

He understands exactly what the Reading costs and believes that knowledge obligates him to ensure it continues.
He does not consider the sacrifice good. He considers it necessary.
Because the Ritual works, he believes the Order possesses both the duty and the authority to administer it. Change may someday be conceivable, but only after an alternative has already been proven safe—an impossible standard that prevents meaningful experimentation.

## Relationship to the Keeper

He treats the Keeper with solemn respect, kindness, and every comfort the Order can provide.
He would spare her if he believed he could.
But he does not believe her refusal—or anyone else’s objection—can outweigh the lives of the entire Colony. Her willingness matters to the meaning of the Reading, but not to whether he will permit it to proceed.

## Relationship to the Party

He sees the Party as armed outsiders who have discovered the Ritual’s cost without understanding its function.
He will hear their argument. He will not permit them to act upon it.
If they attempt to disrupt the Reading, he orders the ritual complex secured and stands between them and the Chamber. He uses restraint while restraint remains possible, but he will kill or die before allowing the only known protection for the Colony to be interrupted.

On the **successful Reading** path, his concern changes shape during Act II. Kelda’s reports repeatedly place Darkwing at sites where forgotten identities, Fractured Objects, records, or Engrams become more coherent after the Party intervenes. As those correlations accumulate, Serevan concludes that Darkwing is reopening a transfer the Reading already completed. His diagnosis is substantially correct. His response is **Closure**: detain and separate Darkwing and isolate the resonant nodes before the reconstructed relationship can become strong enough to reconnect with the Book.

The escalation should make him more intolerant precisely as Darkwing becomes better at Stabilization. From his perspective, their success is evidence of the danger. His familiar blind spot remains intact: because Closure may technically work, he assumes the Order possesses the authority to impose it.

## Relationship to the Captain

He trusts the Captain completely but gives her only the information required to perform her duties.
He believes shielding subordinates from the full moral burden of command is an act of mercy. She believes his secrecy reflects trust in the chain of command—until she learns how much responsibility she was asked to carry without understanding it.

## Institutional Memory

His Prominence joined the Order when it was still more visibly a protective and ritual institution than a government. He has personally witnessed procedures reduce casualties, failed containment produce real harm, and successful Readings restore stability. He cannot identify one moment when emergency authority became permanent rule; each individual expansion of power appeared necessary at the time.

## Branch Trajectory

If the Reading is interrupted, His Prominence becomes the Order’s principal defender of restoration and may be defeated, incapacitated, killed, or politically broken depending on play. If the Reading succeeds, the result vindicates his public position. When the Party begins reconstructing the Forgotten Keeper as a second anchor, he orders an emergency Closure to separate the Party, objects, records, engrams, and relationships before they can reopen the completed inheritance.
