The Colony existed before the Reading. Its institutions grew around a response to danger that worked, then gradually made the continuation of that response inseparable from the continuation of society.

## The Settlement Before the Ritual

Founded under Chelish rule sometime between the Elven-Tongued Conquest and the People's Revolt, the settlement began in the foothills of the Aspodell Mountains. Its original purpose and any earlier name are no longer remembered. Today :ref[the Colony]{#location.colony} stands astride the River Foam, on the border of Isger and Andoran, independent of both.

These missing origins need not conceal another answer the Party must discover. They establish a place whose inhabitants have inherited freedom, obligations, and institutions without remembering every circumstance that produced them.

## From Memorial to Institution

The :ref[Book of Names]{#source.book-of-names} began as a memorial register. Its crowded early pages recorded the dead, disappeared, and unclaimed. :ref[Aletheia]{#npc.aletheia}, a keeper of that register, recognized the names as lives connected to her own and wrote her own name. Her act preceded the office of Keeper and the institution that would preserve it.

The Colony forgot her, but retained the observable sequence that relieved its pressure. When that pressure returned, people repeated the sequence. The succession of Keepers grew out of that repetition. [GM Truths](/docs/running/gm-truths/) explains what actually happens during the Calling, Reading, and eclipse.

:::secret
The :ref[Order of Theia]{#faction.order-of-theia} preserves a fragment of Aletheia's name without remembering the woman. The :ref[Monument of the First Keeper]{#location.monument-of-the-first-keeper} preserves an act without preserving the person who performed it. Their connection is a promise the campaign eventually makes explicit.
:::

## Successive Answers

The Order's caretakers became rulers as safeguarding the ritual expanded into civic administration. The :ref[Witnesses]{#faction.witnesses} began as reformers within the Order, insisting that sacrifice deserved remembrance. Their work became a recognized practice of collecting and preserving what remained.

The :ref[Dawn Assembly]{#faction.dawn-assembly} demanded change beyond remembrance. Its reforms improved daily life and earned it a place within the institutions it challenged. The :ref[Shattered]{#faction.shattered} arose from disillusionment with those reforms: to them, a system able to absorb every objection had to be broken.

Other communities approached the same burden through different means. The :ref[Cult of the Unknown]{#faction.cult-of-the-unknown} sought revelation through encounter and surrender. The :ref[EGRESS Institute]{#faction.egress-institute} turned observation into a sustained technical inquiry. :ref[Aspis]{#faction.aspis} arrived seeking commerce and found that keeping obligations intact required understanding the Colony's impossible absences.

These are overlapping histories, not seven successive governments. Each faction still performs useful work; each risks treating its inherited method as a complete answer. Their entries hold the fuller institutional histories.

## History in Play

Let the players encounter an institution's present usefulness before discovering what it has forgotten. A guard protects someone. A Witness knows how an object was repaired. An Aspis record preserves an obligation after its signatory has vanished. Later evidence changes the meaning of those first meetings without making them false.

Do not give every empty space a secret date and founder. The Colony's traditions are collective adaptations to repeated loss; many beginnings remain uncertain. The campaign's historical revelations should recover people and responsibilities, not turn every custom into a clue with one hidden solution.
