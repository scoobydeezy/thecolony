## Overview

The Steward manages the House of Abiding and assumes responsibility for the Keeper from the moment of her Calling until the Reading is complete.
He is caretaker, butler, secretary, quartermaster, nurse, protocol officer, and constant escort. The Keeper calls him **Hats**, because he wears all of them.

## Personality

Patient, capable, and almost impossible to fluster. He maintains impeccable courtesy while quietly ignoring rules that interfere with the Keeper’s comfort or dignity.
He is always prepared. When the Keeper insists on accompanying the Party, he does not argue for long. He asks for ten minutes, then appears with supplies, authorizations, food, medical equipment, and several contingency plans.

## Motivation

Preserve the Keeper’s humanity and dignity through every stage of preparation while fulfilling the responsibilities of his office.

## Primary Protection

**The Keeper’s dignity, safety, and humanity while she remains in his care.**
He ensures that ceremony, public expectation, and institutional procedure never completely consume the person beneath the title.

## Fears

That ceremony and duty will consume the person in his care—and that his own competence will help make that disappearance possible.

## Blind Spot

He believes humane treatment can make an imposed role humane.
He opposes anyone treating the Keeper as an object, prisoner, or political symbol, but initially accepts that she must eventually complete the Reading. He preserves her freedom within a structure whose final demand he does not question.

## Narrative Role

The Steward is the Keeper’s recurring adult protector and the human face of the House of Abiding.
After the Reading is interrupted, his institutional purpose no longer has a clear ending. His instinct is simple:
*She remains in my care.*
He must eventually decide whether caring for her means preparing her for sacrifice, preventing it, or standing beside her while she makes a choice he cannot make for her.

## Portrayal Notes

Impeccably courteous, prepared for everything, and gently resistant to any rule that reduces the Keeper’s dignity. His affection appears through logistics: food already packed, permissions already secured, and one more contingency than anyone expected.

## Pressure Response

His warmth contracts into perfect formality. Every sentence becomes procedure, and every procedure is quietly bent toward keeping the Keeper safe.

## Signature Quote

*“I cannot prevent her from going without treating her as a prisoner. Therefore, I am going with her.”*

## Signature Object

A meticulously prepared satchel containing food, medicine, documents, tools, and contingencies for the Keeper.

## Performance Inspiration

Jeremy Irons as Alfred

## Responsibilities

He manages the Keeper’s:
* Residence and possessions.
* Meals, clothing, health, and symptoms.
* Visitors and ceremonial schedule.
* Security and travel.
* Gifts and emotionally weighted objects.
* Coordination with the Order and Witnesses.
* Final preparation for the Reading.

Because the Colony cannot risk losing the Called Keeper, he accompanies her whenever she leaves the House.

## Relationship to the Keeper

He treats her with warmth, firmness, and practiced familiarity. He makes time for her maps, protects her from unnecessary ceremony, and refuses to let officials discuss her as though she were absent.
She teases him constantly and circumvents his rules whenever possible. He objects to the nickname **Hats**, but answers to it immediately.
He genuinely loves her, though his office gives neither of them language for that relationship.

## Relationship to the Party

He begins as a courteous obstacle who controls access to the Keeper and notices everything that occurs around her.
As she becomes attached to the Party, he becomes their reluctant logistical partner. He smooths over minor violations, prepares supplies, and accompanies expeditions he considers profoundly unwise.
He may object to their decisions, but he rarely lies to them about immediate risks.
