## Overview

The technician is a gnome engineer responsible for extracting, stabilizing, analyzing, and containing engrams within EGRESS.
They understand better than almost anyone that a corrupted engram is not a preserved person. It is a record of memory attempting—and often failing—to reconstruct one.
This distinction matters enormously to them.
The distinction between voluntary and involuntary containment does not.

## Personality

Pragmatic, literal, and socially unbothered.
They usually communicate in brief, functional statements and make little effort to anticipate how accurate information may affect someone emotionally.
When discussing engrams, containment architecture, or an unusual pattern in the data, they become animated and extraordinarily verbose. They will continue explaining until interrupted, the emergency ends, or someone uses a technically incorrect term.
They are not embarrassed by awkwardness and rarely notice when others are.

## Motivation

Produce technically valid, stable, and interpretable engram analysis and containment, regardless of whether the subjects appreciate the procedure.

## Primary Protection

**The distinction between what the instruments demonstrate and what EGRESS has merely inferred.**
They despise contaminated data, premature classification, and theories that become institutional truths before being properly tested.
They will oppose a convenient false conclusion even when it benefits EGRESS politically.

## Fears

That contaminated methodology or premature classification will create a false subject, destroy interpretable evidence, or overload a system that should have remained stable.

## Blind Spot

They believe correct measurement and competent procedure make an action legitimate.
They understand that EGRESS can misidentify a person. They do not initially understand that identifying someone perfectly does not grant authority over them.
To the technician, a safe and accurately documented procedure is justified by being safe and accurately documented.

## Narrative Role

The technician gives the Party practical access to EGRESS’s methods and becomes the central guide through the Engram Vault or containment breach.
They reveal that memory can be measured, partitioned, protected, and routed—but that technical similarity does not make separate lives interchangeable.
Their expertise may be indispensable to the final transfer.
Their presence also ensures that EGRESS’s most dangerous choices cannot be dismissed as simple scientific ignorance. Sometimes the Institute understands exactly what it is doing and proceeds anyway.

## Portrayal Notes

Usually brief, literal, and unconcerned with social discomfort. Technical interest unlocks sudden torrents of detail. Never play them as timid or innocently oblivious: they understand the confinement and simply regard it as correct procedure.

## Pressure Response

They begin narrating every procedure aloud, including risks, measurements, and corrections, while continuing the containment whether anyone consents or not.

## Signature Quote

*“It is not consuming your identity. It is using your identity to interpolate missing connective material. Those are entirely different processes.”*

## Signature Object

A diagnostic slate crowded with live containment readings and terse corrective annotations.

## Performance Inspiration

Richard Ayoade as Moss, played completely straight

## Engram Expertise

The technician can distinguish between:
* A memory and a reconstruction derived from it.
* Original behavior and behavior produced by containment.
* One person and several people expressing similar emotional patterns.
* A coherent identity and fragments incorrectly classified as one subject.
* Stable containment and a system that simply has not failed yet.
* A genuine relationship and an inference supplied by EGRESS equipment.

They frequently correct researchers who refer to engrams as incomplete personalities.
“That is not the subject. That is the error through which the system attempted to reproduce the subject.”

## Relationship to Containment

The technician has no moral objection to confining someone who carries an uncharacterized mnemonic phenomenon.
Their reasoning is simple:
1. The phenomenon may be transmissible.
2. Its limits are unknown.
3. Other people may be endangered.
4. EGRESS possesses a chamber designed to contain it.
5. Therefore, the subject remains inside the chamber.

Consent is preferable when it does not compromise containment.
They are considerate toward confined subjects. They provide food, comfort, explanations, warnings, and carefully controlled tests.
They do not open the door.
“You are not being punished.”
“Then let us out.”
“No. You are being contained.”

## Relationship to the Party

The technician traps the Party, studies them, explains precisely why they are unusual, and does not apologize for any of it.
They may nevertheless become the Party’s most useful guide within EGRESS.
They recognize when several similar memories have been incorrectly combined, identify capacity limits, and teach the Party how engrams can be separated, protected, and routed without treating them as interchangeable energy.
Eventually, they may be the first researcher to conclude that the Party is not one distributed subject. They are several distinct people connected through one reconstructed relationship.
They accept this because the evidence demands it—not because confinement has taught them respect for autonomy.

## Relationship to the Director

The technician respects the Director’s intelligence, resources, and willingness to fund experiments no other institution would consider.
They object when the Director:
* Exceeds tested capacity.
* Combines incompatible engrams.
* Introduces uncontrolled variables.
* Treats temporary stability as proof.
* Alters methodology to produce a desired result.
* Risks destroying the phenomenon being studied.

Their strongest objections often sound ethical while remaining entirely procedural:
“I have no moral objection to what you are proposing. I am telling you that it will not produce interpretable results.”
The Director finds this distinction useful until the technician’s commitment to valid results prevents the Institute from claiming success.
