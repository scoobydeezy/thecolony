## Overview

The Hall’s Caretaker is a senior Witness and one of the Colony’s foremost experts on Fractured Objects.
She catalogs emotionally weighted possessions, studies their relationships, monitors dangerous resonances, and preserves what can no longer safely remain in passage.
She is the Steward’s wife and the Party’s primary contact within the Witnesses.

## Personality

Precise, composed, and endlessly patient. She never dismisses an emotional response merely because its cause cannot be consciously remembered.
Her empathy is quieter than her husband’s. He comforts people instinctively; she gives their grief the time and attention required to become understandable.

## Motivation

Identify, stabilize, and preserve the relationships embedded in Fractured Objects before they are lost, redistributed, or weaponized.

## Primary Protection

**The relationships preserved within Fractured Objects.**
She believes every dangerous object contains evidence that someone mattered to someone else, even when neither person can still be identified.

## Fears

That a relationship will be lost, misidentified, or weaponized because she failed to preserve the evidence that someone mattered.

## Blind Spot

She believes preservation is sufficient.
She can protect the evidence of a forgotten relationship without asking whether the living should be allowed to recover, acknowledge, or materially respond to that relationship.
The Hall remembers what remains, but rarely changes the world that produced the absence.

## Narrative Role

The Caretaker gives the Party access to the Witnesses’ methods, records, and understanding of Fractured Objects.
She demonstrates that remembrance requires patience and care, while also revealing the limits of memory preserved only inside an archive. Her work saves lives, but its competence also allows the Colony to continue forgetting without confronting what has been lost.

## Portrayal Notes

Precise, composed, and patient enough to let an emotion become evidence without rushing to explain it. Her empathy is analytical but never cold. She treats objects with the care another person would give a frightened witness.

## Pressure Response

She becomes more clinical and directive, separating people and objects before emotion can contaminate the pattern she is trying to preserve.

## Signature Quote

*“The object is not the person. But it may still show us where the person used to be.”*

## Signature Object

A pair of object-handling gloves marked with tiny catalog notations from years of careful use.

## Performance Inspiration

Olivia Colman

## Expertise

She understands that Fractured Objects can:
* Trigger memory without context.
* Draw particular people toward them.
* Transfer distress between handlers.
* Become focal points for Veiled manifestations.
* Be stabilized through careful handling, records, separation, or reconnection.

She knows that the Hall’s collection grows after every Reading, but does not understand that many of the newest objects belonged to the forgotten Keeper.

## Relationship to the Steward

Their work forms two halves of the same process.
He cares for the living Keeper and knows the meaning of every object in the House. After the Reading, he finds possessions belonging to no one and transfers them to the Hall.
She receives the objects and preserves the relationships within them.
Neither remembers the person whose life they have just divided into catalog entries.

## Relationship to the Keeper

The Keeper is fascinated by her work and by the incomplete stories attached to the Hall’s collection.
The Caretaker takes the girl’s observations seriously, especially her belief that an inaccurate representation may still reveal where understanding failed. She may be the first adult to recognize the usefulness of describing the Veiled as **bad maps of people**.

## Relationship to the Party

She becomes the Party’s practical Witnesses liaison.
She can help them trace an object, identify emotional patterns, access preserved records, and understand what a manifestation is attempting to reconstruct. She offers evidence and interpretation without pretending to possess complete answers.

## Act III — Witnesses Under Totality (GM Only)

Celia assembles twelve credible archival candidates and an authentic field procedure, then becomes vulnerable to a self-aware Spider whose deception exploits the Hall's legitimate scientific method. She is **not** incompetent or complicit. Her instinct is to quarantine new unprovenanced mnemonic material, but she changes her conclusion if Darkwing produces contradictory evidence. Preserve her agency as an archivist rather than making her solve or refuse to solve the encounter by script.
