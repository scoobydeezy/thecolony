## At the Table

**Objective:** locate Sundara’s actual grave and sound its whistle. **Board:** the cemetery prototype below; four individual Sundara slots plus the Troop. **Information:** individual Dispersals flare the nearest grave; Troop damage gives no probe. **Finish:** a truthful Name boundary ends the mass false Sundaras, while Old Town’s larger Weight remains.

## Mechanical Reference

Use :ref[Veiled Sundara]{#creature.veiled-sundara} and :ref[the Shambler Troop]{#creature.veiled-shambler-troop}. Earlier Named graves are legitimate calibration points, not retroactive rewards for an unplayed route.

### Narrative Container — What Actually Happened

**The original Old Town disaster was a Veiled zombie outbreak, not merely a cemetery expanding.** Forgotten lives accumulated in hundreds of original Sources—grave offerings, flowers, cherished objects, burial records, and occasionally living relationships. When the cemetery attempted to "remember the dead," the available cultural Form was **dead people returning to life**. The result was a mass of perfectly black, corporeal zombie-shaped Incursions. The Broken Bell rang from the old watchtower. The Colony's defenders could not hold Old Town; they **burned the district and destroyed Source vessels** to stop the attacks, deliberately sacrificing much of the original town and its defensive wall. Dispersal stopped the immediate fighting, but the destruction caused **Rupture and Spill**. Raw Weight with nowhere suitable to go became Scars, and accumulating environmental Context matured into the enduring Old Town **Wound**. No source destruction erased Weight, and the catastrophe was never truly resolved.

Over decades the Wound gained compatible Recognition through the surviving groundskeeper, **Tomlin Cobb**. Tom is now the **dominant living Source / Recognition focus of the *current* mass manifestation**, not the original source of every grave and not a receptacle holding all residual Weight. Other original, scarred, and Anchored Sources remain distributed throughout Old Town. His Fracturing preserves relationships, places, habits, and duties while personal **Names** slide into the same damaged association: *Sunny*. The older outbreak mistakenly reconstructed **many different dead people**. Under Totality, Tom's unresolved search for one woman makes **hundreds of incorrect reconstructions of the *same* Sundara**.

**Encounter identity:** A desperate, visually unmistakable **zombie-slasher horde battle combined with a cooperative cemetery deduction game**. The party must reach the cemetery, learn that the individual zombies are all false Sundaras, deliberately Disperse them in informative places, find **one correct grave**, and sound its whistle. They do **not** defeat or fully cleanse Old Town.

### Totality — Opening Image and Situation

The **real ruined masonry stays in place**. Every missing wall, roof, house, arch, and section of the original Colony wall resumes its former shape as a **solid, featureless, absolute-black continuation** of the ruin. The watchtower that once held the real, damaged **Broken Bell** returns in black around it; the bell rings continually as the historic alarm replays. Some smaller environmental reconstruction errors survive independently: the bottomless oversized well is a good optional example; keep those as local hazards rather than mandatory puzzles. These structures are not phantasms. Old and present routes can overlap, but this encounter is **not another Milltown rotating-tile labyrinth**.

A Troop-sized mass of black Sundaras pushes through the ruined streets and cemetery fences. A smaller number of individually tracked Sundaras roam inside the graveyard. Their bodies vary in incorrect ancestral Form and anatomical details, but all are attempts at **one missing identity**. Tom, near the oldest graves, continues calling them **Sunny**—and they react. Use the call to telegraph the party's possible lever before they deliberately try it.

Old Town is a **site within Totality, not a mandatory stop or one faction's exclusive quest**. Before the session, read the actual champion and Asset state from :ref[the faction rules]{#rule.faction-goals}. The Order, Witnesses, Shattered, Cult, and nearby facilities may have simultaneous goals here, but give each a concrete objective and deadline **only when campaign state supports it**. The undead-looking Wound threatens all of them; solving its mass manifestation can preserve capabilities but does not automatically repair the Ledger, Pilgrim's Rest, Dead Drop Network, or Hall of the Keepers.

### Earlier Old Town Route — What It Does and Does Not Give Away

If Darkwing completed **Old Town** in Act II, it has **six individually restored lives and six known, Anchored grave locations**. Put six Anchor markers on those six plots at the start of Sunny. They are legitimate, exceptionally useful **directional calibration points** in the new grave-search mechanic. Their previously authored Names remain canonical. The Party may know the cemetery's identity grammar and Tom's Name-fracture, but **did not discover Sundara's gravestone, the fragment `SUNDA—`, the whistle, or her full Name**.

If it skipped Old Town, these six plot Anchors are **not granted retroactively**; no required information is gated behind that investigation. The party can discover Grave Resonance through the first accidental Dispersal, use ordinary probes, and reach the same solution. Do not repeat the six-Corpus fragment-swap fight inside Sunny.

### What the Players Discover — In Play, Not in Exposition

1. **Survive the horde.** The first individual Veiled Sundara is Dispersed in ordinary combat. Ask for a **Perception check** to notice that the *nearest gravestone* vibrates or gathers a brief black thread of Recognition. If missed, repeat the tell on the next obvious Dispersal; discovery must not depend on passing one hidden check.
2. **Recognize the repeated mistake.** Tom addresses disparate-looking zombies as "Sunny". Every one responds. Unlike the previous six mismatched lives, these are attempts at **the same person**. Darkwing can discover this through observation, calling the Name, Recall Knowledge, or Tom's instinctive responses; it need not learn the exact real Name to start testing graves.
3. **Experiment with placement.** Any individually tracked zombie can be Dispersed anywhere on the board. **The nearest gravestone to the zombie at the instant of Dispersal is the one tested.** Do not require Grapple, grave contact, or an invented test attack. Ranged fire, reach, movement, terrain, barriers, and calling Sunny all matter.
4. **Interpret a resonating grave.** A participant who can perceive the flare uses **one action to Recall Knowledge** and receives the degree-based deterministic feedback specified in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page. Mark information on the physical map. Ordinary wrong graves cost actions/exposure, but **do not spawn extra zombies just because they are wrong**.
5. **Use previous Anchors if available.** Dispersal whose nearest stone is one of the six Old Town Anchors yields **guaranteed direction** toward Sundara, regardless of the interpretation result; the normal check may add its own distance band. This requires choosing to operate near known Anchors; it is not a passive numerical bonus.
6. **Find the correct stone.** A Dispersal keyed to Sundara's grave always yields an unmistakably different convergence, **even on a failed interpretation**. Examine it: the carved swallow and recess contain a small ceramic **swallow whistle**, and its damaged inscription reads **`SUNDA—`**. That inscription and object have been hidden until this moment.
7. **Blow the whistle.** Any PC can sound it. Every individual Sundara and the Troop **freeze**, recognizing an authentic Pattern associated with the actual woman. Tom recognizes the sound, walks through the immobile figures to the grave, sees `SUNDA—`, closes his eyes, and speaks: **"Sundara."** No final Diplomacy check, guessed password, or additional Name roll is required.

**Do not have the zombies whistle.** The whistle is the *confirmation and trigger*, not a repetitive tell that gives away the grave. The shared tell is **their response to "Sunny"**, noticed in their behavior and Tom's speech. The grave search itself supplies the means of finding the object.

### Grave Resonance — Player-Facing Investigation Rules

The party finds the *actual* grave through repeated physical measurements rather than a hidden biographical Name check. Every independent Sundara is an incomplete Form of the same identity; thus every probe points toward the same true grave. The horde Troop is not a container of separate measurable bodies and **does not produce a probe** when segments die. Do not run separate Source puzzles for each zombie.

- **Select the stone deterministically:** nearest gravestone measured from the zombie's Dispersal square. For the paper prototype use **Manhattan grid distance** `|row1-row2| + |col1-col2|` solely to select and measure graves (not PF2e movement). Break ties by the lowest grave ID. Walls and fences don't alter the abstract Resonance distance.
- **Flare:** the selected stone flashes with a low, dark vibration. One flare lasts until the **end of the following round**. Multiple bodies Dispersed by one attack may generate multiple flares but do not require GM measurements until players choose to interpret them. A single flare is readable once; later new Dispersals can retest a grave for more precise information at a real combat cost.
- **Interpret:** 1-action **Recall Knowledge**, secret, visible flare in range (60-foot line of sight); skills may include Occultism, Religion, or Society when grounded in the evidence. The correct grave's object/reaction is evident **without a check**. Exact check DCs, timing and exceptional effects are in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page.
- **Critical success:** eight-way direction **plus** distance band to Sundara's true grave.
- **Success:** distance band only.
- **Failure:** **warmer / colder / equal** than the *previous distinct grave tested* (compare true distances, not prior die results). If this is the first test, there is no previous comparison; the party still knows the tested grave is wrong.
- **Critical failure:** no extra reading; the tested grave is still known not to be the correct one.
- **Distance bands:** `0–4`, `5–8`, `9–12`, `13–18`, `19+` grid squares; **do not reveal an exact distance**. A direction names the cardinal or diagonal sector defined by the signs of row/column differences. Record tested graves, eliminated candidates, distance bands and directions; never give a false direction on a bad roll.
- **Old Town calibration:** When one of the six prior Anchored graves is the selected stone, grant **direction automatically**, even on failure or critical failure. An ordinary check may additionally reveal distance band; the automatic direction is not an independent skill roll and does not replace the physical Dispersal.

### Calling Sunny — A Lever, Not Control

Tom calling "Sunny" makes every zombie react; the GM should show this as soon as the party can observe it. A PC may deliberately call **"Sunny!"** to attract the four individual manifestations **and the Troop** within hearing. Treat the focused call as a simple, one-action, auditory **encounter activity**; it sets a target, not guaranteed movement, dominance, a mind-control condition, or an instant grave test. They still navigate the actual fences, attacks and terrain and can attack a PC who obstructs them. Only the first effective call in a round sets the priority until the end of the targets' next turns; later calls that round do not ping-pong them. Details in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page.

This should be discoverable from the fiction. A team might lure Sundaras across an informative sector, use ranged attacks to choose their Dispersal squares, and spend an investigator's action reading the flare. A party that keeps killing zombies in one location will keep seeing **the same nearest stones**, nudging it to change position. Combat is the instrument of investigation, not a separate puzzle mode.

### Two-Layer Threat and Battlefield Rhythm

**Layer 1 — The Horde:** one active **Veiled Shambler Troop**, represented under **Remaster troop segmentation** by up to four contiguous 10×10-foot segments. It presses through burial avenues, damages fences when appropriate, and makes standing still dangerous. It is **not undead**: zombie Form does not confer the Undead trait, void healing, or necromancy interactions. Use the the mechanical reference entries linked on this page custom reskin and balance to actual party level. No Troop segment yields a grave reading.

**Layer 2 — Individuals:** begin with **four** comparatively fragile, independent tactical bodies within the **same mass Chorus**, not four new Sources. They are valid probe instruments; they pursue/attack PCs, and a deliberate call can redirect their priority. When Dispersed, their slots reform at fixed cemetery emergence points on a rapid cycle until Tom's Name boundary is restored. No need for the party to Grapple or shove a body into contact with a stone.

**Two-layer bookkeeping:** track one Troop with segment/HP thresholds, four individual slots (active/Dispersed/reconstructing), fence condition only when attacked, and a map of *interpreted* readings. Don't resolve 100 incidental flares. A proposed **Break from the Horde** optional ability (lost Troop segment -> one individual slot) is in the mechanical reference entries linked on this page, but is **not a prerequisite** and must not create a fifth independent body or fresh Weight.

**GM pacing:** ordinary attacks, repositioning, fencing, luring, and investigative readings consume regular Pathfinder actions. A first incidental flare is an ordinary Perception clue, not a novel PC action. Give enough behavioral cues to enable the party to understand "Sunny" and move around the board; don't hand them the correct grave or secretly change it to end the encounter. Prior simulation of naive agents ran long, but the group has a GM who can provide diegetic feedback without invalidating tests. Favor meaningful tactical progress over artificial turn or kill quotas.

### The Moment of Restoration

When the whistle sounds, the horde freezes. Tom walks through it to the matched grave, recognizes the object and damaged inscription, and remembers his wife's actual Name: **Sundara Cobb**. The genuine **Name + Relationship** boundary separates the one woman from the hundreds of incorrect reconstructions using her place. The mass Chorus **Stabilizes**; its bodies cease and do not reconstruct during this crisis. The Broken Bell stops ringing.

**Do not call this full Old Town Resolution.** Tom is still Fractured, and the district retains environmental Wound Weight, Scars, altered buildings, independent grave and object Sources, the bottomless well, and potentially the reopened watchtower. The six lives Restored during Old Town stay Restored, not reabsorbed. Sundara's name recovery need not cure Tom's general Name impairment, establish unlimited Anchor Capacity, or dispose of every original forgotten life. The whistle is **an authentic Fractured Object and Recognition key**, not a magic command artifact, and its recovered custody should be recorded as a genuine Asset/Source change if it matters later.

If Tom is harmed, forcibly removed, or killed **before** Restoration, do not arbitrarily complete the scene: Source destruction risks Rupture and uncontrolled Spill, with terrifying Scar consequences under the universal rules. Set the actual outcome from play. The default win is ending the *current* mass-Sundara reconstruction without murdering its living Source.

### Act III → Finale Handoff (No New Universal Pressure Meter)

**Sunny is the Shattered front.** Its one outcome is simultaneously the **Dead Drop Network finale capability** and the **+2/+1/+0 Finale Pressure contribution**: **Stable** means Darkwing or a capable delegate truthfully stabilizes the mass-Sundara Incursion and preserves effective Shattered response; **Damaged** means a partial or resource-degraded but useful response survives; **Lost** means the unchecked crisis leaves no effective Shattered intervention. The older Old Town Wound and Scars may remain even after a Stable result; a front measures the crisis response, not complete restoration of an entire district.

Record direct intervention, supported delegation, or the unattended deadline as the route to this **single** outcome. Do not automatically award Stable for a visit or reduce the score because other unrelated factions lost Assets. The same crisis can still change other NPCs, locations, and projects as separate events, without creating another Shattered Asset-status roll.

## Grave Resonance and Calling

## Grave Resonance — Trigger and Investigation

**Trigger:** An *individual* Sundara is reduced to 0 HP and **Disperses**. The **single nearest grave** to its last occupied square resonates (the corpses of real townspeople are not being consumed). Use Manhattan coordinate distance purely for this **investigation measurement**, not PF2e movement or attack range. With equal distance, use the lowest numbered grave. Number the 71 stones north-to-south, west-to-east. An NPC/non-PC dispersal can also cause a flare; only a player's observed or investigated flare becomes an actionable reading. **Troop segment losses and Troop Dispersal never produce 4–16 grave tests.**

**Tell:** First observed individual Dispersal, ask PCs who can see the stone for a **GM-triggered Perception check** (moderate DC for party level; **DC 30** for level 12) to notice a nearby stone tremble and a black thread converge on it. The actual check does not cost them an action. On an initial failure, repeat the visible phenomenon on a later Dispersal; it is a tutorial, not a secret gate or one-chance clue. Once discovered, no further Perception rolls are required for ordinary flares.

**Duration:** Flare can be read through the **end of the next combat round**. An aware PC who has line of sight to the flare from **within 60 feet** may spend **one action to Recall Knowledge**, using Occultism, Society, Religion, or another genuinely relevant skill. Treat as a **secret check**, **DC 30** for a level-12 group (no automatic bonus for the name being obvious in roleplay). Choose appropriate level-based DC and difficulty adjustments for actual party. One reading per flare; a new individual Dispersal permits a new reading at that same stone, though its true spatial result cannot change.

| Degree | Information actually provided |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | **Compass direction + distance band** from the responding grave toward the actual Sundara grave. Direction uses north/south/east/west and diagonals, from the signs of row/column difference; no exact coordinate. |
| **Success** | **Distance band only**, using Manhattan grid squares: `0–4`, `5–8`, `9–12`, `13–18`, `19+`. Never give the exact number. |
| **Failure** | **Warmer / colder / equal** relative to the *previous distinct tested grave*, comparing their actual Manhattan distances to Sundara. The GM can make the comparison even if the previous skill reading was bad. If this is the first distinct test, there is no comparison; only the negative result is known. |
| **Critical failure** | No additional measurement. Because the correct grave always unmistakably answers, an ordinary non-matching test still logically excludes its own grave. No false data. |

**Correct grave overrides dice:** If the resonating grave is the pre-keyed Sundara grave (paper layout **#10, r04 c20**), the resonance is **unique and obvious even on a failed or unread check**. A PC who investigates the matching grave finds a small **ceramic swallow whistle** in a recess, a worn swallow carving, and the broken inscription **`SUNDA—`**. This is the **only** place/time the player-facing plot reveals those specifics. The object is an authentic Fractured Object connected to her and Tom; the whistle is **not** a free magic Name-detection tool usable at arbitrary graves.

**Old Town Anchor calibration:** If the party truly completed the six-life Old Town encounter, its six previously Anchored graves are marked on the GM and player map. Whenever the selected responding stone is one of those six, grant a **correct compass direction** toward Sundara *for free regardless of the check's degree*; a successful or critically successful Recall Knowledge may supply its normal **distance band** as well. A failure may still provide warmer/colder if the group wants it, but the automatic direction is the essential payoff. Six pre-eliminated graves alone were insignificant in simulation; these are **active reference locations** that the party must choose to use. Skipping Sunny provides no calibration; the remaining investigation works normally.

**No wrong-stone reinforcements:** Wrong probes cost combat positioning, attacks, and reader actions. They do **not** cause special extra summons. Previous simulation found that extra zombies often paradoxically made it easier to find a measurement. The Troop and four body slots already create ongoing pressure.

**Skip redundant GM work:** An incidental kill produces a visible reaction but **not automatically a die roll or a clue report**. If ten bodies are Dispersed beside one stone, the GM only resolves a measurement when somebody deliberately interprets a flare. A repeated test is valid but the underlying spatial truth never changes.

## Calling Sunny ◆ — Focus Recognition

**One action; auditory; encounter-specific.** The creature loudly calls "Sunny!" with the deliberate intent of drawing the reconstructions' attention. Treat this as a one-action focus of the Wound's Name-Recognition rather than a new general Diplomacy or Intimidation action. **All four individuals and the Troop within 60 feet that can hear it** prioritize the caller as a target until the end of their next turn. The first effective call in a round sets target priority; further calls that round do not oscillate the attention. Ordinary short conversation is still free/narrative, but **the mechanical lure consumes the one action**.

A called Sundara still uses ordinary PF2e movement and attacks, and may attack a creature actively blocking its route. Calling does not teleport it, require it to approach a particular grave, override fences, or render it harmless. The attraction is to the **misrecognized Name and speaker**, not to an investigator's declared grave. Tom's initial call provides the **diegetic tutorial**. The party can choose its position and attack timing to make the **nearest grave** at death informative.

## Terrain and Rest

## Wrought-Iron Fence / Optional Local Wound Reference

**Fence:** edge terrain, normally blocks passage across that edge; lattice does not categorically block vision, but grants cover when appropriate. Ordinary creature Athletics, object damage, Climb, Fly, teleportation, and line-of-effect rules work as usual. Broad gaps accommodate four-segment Troop geometry; narrow breaks can serve as individual shortcuts. **Final segment HP/Hardness and exact edge coordinates remain to be designed** with the physical map. Do not silently assume the Troop can flow through barred walls simply because it is a supernatural reconstruction.

**Bottomless well:** optional localized Wound, large obvious hole in the floor. Its fatal drop or other danger must be telegraphed with actual terrain coordinates and appropriate falling/forced movement counterplay. It is **not** Sundara's grave, not a resonance source, and not a shortcut to full Old Town Resolution. Use only if it improves the battlefield rather than adding a fourth mandatory puzzle.

## Ending — Source and Rest

**Whistle:** Any creature may **Interact ◆** to bring the recovered ceramic swallow whistle to its mouth and sound it (or adjudicate routine item handling already established in the scene). If the sound can be heard by Tom and the active mass (run it as a scene-wide Pattern Recognition when sounded within the cemetery), every active Sundara and the Troop immediately stop attacking. Tom walks to the true grave, sees the damaged `SUNDA—` carving, and recognizes the complete Name, **Sundara**. No final roleplay-success threshold, Diplomacy roll, or separate Recall Knowledge gate. If Tom is unavailable or cannot hear, re-establish an actual audible/Recognition pathway rather than firing the effect remotely through arbitrary walls.

**Mechanics:** Restored Name + Relationship establishes the truth of the one woman and makes the false mass Chorus unviable. Remove active individuals and the Troop, cancel reconstruction timers, and stop Broken Bell's historical alarm; **do not remove** every smaller environmental Source, black reconstructed structure, Scar, the Wound, or Tom's persistent Fracturing. This is **Stabilization of the current manifestation**, not universal Anchoring/Rest for Old Town. Tom remains a living Source of the larger unresolved district burden unless an actual subsequent transfer/Rest occurs.

**Serious failure:** If Tom's Source is actually destroyed, use the general Rupture and uncontrolled Spill rules from the Veiled rules, including plausible new Scars and more severe Wound behavior. Do not hand-wave this into success because the zombie bodies disappear.
