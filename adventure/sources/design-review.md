# Design criteria retained during migration

These are authoring and playtest criteria, not a second mechanical rules reference. Current rule owners and the user’s six-Keystone ruling govern. Historical open-work lists are reconciled in MIGRATION-REVIEW.md. Source exports are not needed to read this record.

## Veiled encounter design

### GM Principles

When designing or running a Veiled encounter:

- **Design the weight before designing the monster.**
- **Ask what is recognizing what. Topology creates mechanics.**
- **The Veiled is not the Forgotten.**
- **A Source carries weight; it does not contain a ghost.**
- **Resonance is Recognition made mechanical.**
- **Breaking Resonance suppresses the problem; it does not make the reconstruction truthful.**
- **Class measures pressure, not creature level or moral importance.**
- **Use normal Pathfinder rules wherever they already solve the problem.**
- **Damage Disperses reconstruction; it does not destroy weight.**
- **The Stabilization Window runs in normal encounter time; every non-movement action, reaction, and free action advances Reconstruction Pressure.**
- **At least one viable Restoration path must be recoverable through local evidence, prior Assets, faction help, or encounter-time discovery.**
- **Keystones grant reusable access, not automatic answers.**
- **Recovering truth and Restoring truth are different events.**
- **Restored Identity permanently constrains later reconstructions.**
- **Reconstruction and Resonance expose alternate paths to sufficient Meaning; they are not universal double locks.**
- **Player authorship is Acceptance, not arbitrary invention.**
- **Acceptance should end hostile reconstruction without another roll.**
- **Stabilization is not Anchoring.**
- **A Source can become an Anchor once its relationship to the recovered life is coherent.**
- **A legitimate Anchor needs Connection, Capacity, and Consent where agency exists.**
- **Containment can save lives without answering where weight ultimately belongs.**
- **Destroying a Vessel creates Spill, not annihilation.**
- **If weight has nowhere to go, the place carries it and can become a Scar.**
- **A Scar is raw environmental Weight, not a conventional Incursion: its Horizon incorporates coherent lives, while its Boundary can repel or—through genuine keyed Recognition—draw reconstructions without automatically taking their Sources.**
- **A multi-phase Veiled becomes more truthful, not merely stronger.**
- **Humanizing the past is not the same as absolving it.**
- **Every Veiled encounter should teach something the finale can reuse.**
- **If the encounter cannot be reduced to readable table state, it is not finished.**
- **Author evidence and constraints; leave room for the players to synthesize the person those facts imply.**
- **Aletheia is not the final villain. The false reconstruction around her is.**
- **The final reconstruction answers who she was. The final campaign decision answers where the weight rests.**

### Common Design Failure Modes

## Too Much Player Vocabulary

Do not teach every metaphysical term as a new game rule.

Players mainly need Source, Resonance, Incursion, Class, Disperse, Stabilize, Contain, and Anchor.

Recognition, Meaning, Acceptance, topology, Fragment, Spill, and Capacity can emerge naturally as the campaign makes them relevant.

## The Incursion Is Just a Monster with Extra Steps

The Source and Resonance topology should materially affect the battlefield.

If players can ignore the Source, deal damage, and complete an unrelated ritual after combat, the subsystem is not doing enough work.

## Killing the Resonant Civilian Solves Everything

Do not make living participants function like batteries unless the specific Resonance condition truly depends upon an active biological or emotional state.

Relationships survive death.

The First Law should prevent convenient erasure.

## Class Replaces Encounter Level

Class is not a substitute for PF2e level.

Use normal encounter math for tactical difficulty.

Use Class for mnemonic magnitude.

## Stabilization Windows Become Fifty Actions

Do not let a Dispersal create free downtime. Normal encounter sequencing continues, and every non-movement action, reaction, or free action advances Reconstruction Pressure.

## The Window Is Pure Random Denial

The random push should create uncertainty without making meaningful investigation impossible.

High Class should be more forceful, but the curve must still permit the encounter's required Identity work.

Tune the die and thresholds through play.

## Source Destruction Becomes Optimal

Rupture must be dangerous and topologically costly.

The lesson should be “know where the weight is going,” not “destroy every haunted object before combat begins.”

## Source Destruction Creates a Softlock

Spill changes the problem rather than deleting necessary evidence from the campaign.

If an essential Source is destroyed, provide a coherent consequence: transfer, Scar, redistributed fragments, or another route to the required truth.

## Keystones Become Automatic Keys

Owning the Book should not make every Name trivial.

Require enough evidence to ask the artifact a meaningful question.

## Investigation Stops Mattering After Upgrades

Context remains local. Choice evidence still matters even when the Dawn Charter supplies reusable access.

Keystone checks also become easier as the party exposes more evidence, so battlefield investigation remains valuable throughout the campaign.

## Correct Identity Produces No Feedback

Every Restore should visibly alter the manifestation or topology.

If players cannot tell that truth has changed reality, Identity becomes a hidden checklist.

## Phase Changes Reset Progress

Restored Identity is permanent.

A later reconstruction must incorporate what the Party already corrected.

## Reconstruction and Resonance Are Only Flavor

Recognition of Echo, Chorus, Composite, Wound, Relationship, Pattern, Choice, and Form must predict something tactically useful.

## One Skill Solves Every Manifestation

No universal memory skill.

Use the specific fiction, evidence, and character abilities available.

## Acceptance Becomes a Skill Check

Do not follow “Who were they?” with one last die roll that can invalidate the answer.

The table has already earned the authorship moment through play.

## Anchoring Becomes Puzzle Number Two

Prepare Anchor prerequisites before Acceptance whenever possible.

If a valid Anchor exists, let the weight settle automatically.

If none exists, end the fight anyway and let the problem become political, relational, or logistical.

## Every Source Needs a Different Anchor

The original Source may become the Anchor after Stabilization.

Do not create unnecessary extra objects merely to satisfy a subsystem.

## Consent and Capacity Collapse Together

A willing person can be overwhelmed.

A capable person can refuse.

Preserve both dimensions.

## Containment Is Treated as Failure

Containment is a genuine success when the immediate objective is preventing deaths or buying time.

Its incompleteness is philosophical and long-term, not evidence that the Order is incompetent.

## Recognition Becomes Ceremonial

Major Pattern and Choice encounters should often change ownership, policy, resources, authority, place, or civic responsibility.

Acknowledgment without consequence should not automatically create a truthful Anchor.

## Aletheia Answers the Campaign

Recovering the person who began the inheritance does not grant the GM authority to provide the correct ending through her.

Aletheia restores the forgotten question.

The players still build the answer.

## Encounter review questions

### Final Design Test

Before calling a Veiled encounter complete, ask:

- What carries the Weight?
- What is the Source Level and Class?
- What is the Resonance Type?
- What is the Incursion Trigger?
- What is the Reconstruction Condition?
- What happens if that condition is broken while the Incursion remains active?
- Can players observe and manipulate the topology?
- What false conclusion does reality draw from true but incomplete information?
- Why is that conclusion dangerous now?
- What does combat accomplish besides dealing damage?
- What happens at Dispersal?
- How does the Stabilization Window create urgency?
- Which Identity paths can become sufficiently truthful in this encounter?
- What evidence remains stable enough for the Party to reason from?
- How does Restoration visibly change reality?
- What authorship space is intentionally left to the players?
- What concise recognition or Acceptance makes the false reconstruction untenable?
- Can the original Source become an Anchor?
- If not, what destinations possess Connection, Capacity, and Consent?
- What happens if the Party Contains rather than Resolves the Weight?
- What happens if the Source Ruptures?
- If a Scar is present, where is its Horizon, what happens at its Boundary, and does any keyed attraction move the Incursion, the Source, or both?
- What does this encounter teach that can reappear during Aletheia?
- How is the Colony different afterward?

Then perform the **table-reduction smoke test**:

> **Can the important state be represented clearly with minis, tokens, cards, zones, tracks, or a few spoken labels?**

Players should be able to tell:

- what exists;
- what stays stable;
- what changed;
- what they can affect;
- what truth they are trying to establish.

Custom props are optional.

Readability is not.

Most importantly:

> **Does the encounter feel like the same law that governs the Reading, expressed at a smaller scale?**

If the answer is yes, the Veiled are no longer a separate monster subsystem.

They are the campaign's central truth made playable.

## Campaign review questions

### Final Design Test
Before calling the outline complete, every major campaign component should pass these questions:

- Does it exist because someone is trying to accomplish something?
- Does it reveal truth through experience before explanation?
- Does it preserve both a faction's insight and its blind spot?
- Does a route teach a mechanic that can matter during The Burden?
- Does optional content reward investigation without becoming a mandatory key?
- Does faction hostility create complications rather than missing content?
- Does the finale reconstruct people rather than moral abstractions?
- Does Aletheia restore the forgotten question rather than provide the answer?
- Does the final system emerge from Assets, relationships, and capabilities actually preserved in play?
- Does the ending leave the Colony changed without declaring the Party's answer perfect?

Most importantly:

> **The campaign succeeds when the Party stops asking which faction has the answer and begins asking how the Colony can preserve the responsibility to keep questioning its own.**


## Remaining tuning

Capacity and partition behavior, Scar maturation and interfaces, apparatus-specific routing, suppression duration and failure modes, front deadlines, and numerical encounter balance require playtesting. Universal Source formulas stay in their rule owners; this record does not maintain copies. The finale still needs table templates, physical props, selected Memory Cascade images, and performance refinement.
